—— BLOCK 01 · WEEK-BY-WEEK ——

Ten weeks, one closing segment.

Lidingöloppet is done — 2:27:15, time lost to a congested start and a surrendered final climb, not a blow-up in between. The next ten weeks rebuild toward PRT 100 km on Dec 4: a recovery week, four rebuild weeks taking volume from 60 to ~82 km with the Friday long growing to 40 km, a peak ramp (~93 then 105–115 km) carrying the sub-9 pacing gate, then a two-step taper into race week. The specific work lives entirely inside the Friday long's closing segment — 30 to 90 minutes at HR 142–148 (ceiling 150), 100 km effort. Everything else stays easy. Shins are the one thing that broke last year's build at this exact point in the calendar; the shoe rotation below exists so that doesn't repeat.

Updated Sep 27: Saturday Oct 3 becomes Förbifartspremiären, a one-off 12 km run by feel; and weeks 1–3 pick up an optional sixth day — a Monday commute-home run, easy and on top of the week's target, filling the block's second off day while Wednesday stays the buffer before Kundo. Week 3's version is the watch item: it pushes the week near 100 km, last year's shin-injury volume, so it only runs if the shins have stayed silent.

Updated Oct 11: week 2 (Oct 12–18) loses its Tuesday Kundo slot. The quality session moves to Monday Oct 12 as a solo 4×4 at VO2, heavy strength takes Tuesday with no run, and Thursday’s maintenance lift is dropped so Friday’s 30 km with the last 30 min at HR 145–150 stays the protected key session. Kundo 4×4s run as VO2 from now on.

Updated Oct 16: Friday’s first closing segment is in — block-1 baseline: Oct 16, Sollentunavallen track, 6.14 km in 30:51 at 5:02/km for HR 147.7 (the reference for Oct 23 and Oct 30 on the same track; not directly comparable to the sub-9 road gate). The run was 30.9 km entirely in the Evo SL Silver, which the shoe rule bars from long runs, so the week-3 Monday sixth-day run is withdrawn: Mon Oct 19 is off and week 3 runs the five-day grid, ~85 km. The option returns for discussion at the Nov 1 review. Fri Oct 23’s preamble aims at the low half of Z2, HR 140–145, before the 45 min closing segment.

Updated Oct 25 (weekly review): the race-effort segment band moves from HR 145–150 to 142–148, ceiling 150. Fri Oct 23 ran 40 km on the track (30 km at HR ~130, then 10 km at HR 144 and 5:03/km where HR sat at 147) — level with Oct 16 after 8 km more running and level-to-ahead of the same run in 2025; Patrik said 144 was the effort he could see holding for 100 km, so the prescription follows. Thursday is rewritten as a 14–16 km medium-long at HR ≤145 with strides at km 3 (three Thursdays ran steady without strides; the early slot plus a 20-min watch alert is the fix). Race-shoe rule relaxes to two Friday segments plus the Nov 13 gate run — the Oct 30 segment must be in a candidate (Vanish if kept, else Evo SL Silver). Week 4 holds at ~80–85 km with a second 40 km; week 5 steps to ~92–95 km with a 42 km long, conditional on the Nov 1 check (otherwise hold at ~85).

Updated Oct 22: no down week. Only two build weeks (65 and 72 km) have followed Mauerweglauf, and last year’s shins broke on a 79 → 101 km step, so a 70 km week before a +35–55% jump is the bigger risk. Fri Oct 23 grows to 35–36 km, all on the Sollentunavallen track with a friend (last 45 min at HR 145–150, 60 g/h). Week 4 becomes a steady ~80–85 km week with a 38–40 km long on Oct 30; the peak becomes a ramp, ~92–95 km (40 km long) then 105–115 km (45 km B2B). Shin protection moves from a calendar down week to a symptom rule — any shin signal drops the extra easy runs first, the Friday long stays — plus rotation shoes for all easy volume.

WEEK 6 · NOV 9–15

The peak · week 6

A ramp, not a jump: week 5 (card above) ~92–95 km with a 42 km long, week 6 105–115 km with a 45 km back-to-back long (ceiling set by the Nov 1 check and the Nov 8 review on shins and the long runs). Ramp 65 → 72 → 85 → ~82 → ~93 → ~110, no week more than ~20% above those before it. The second long is a back-to-back with the daughter's Saturday run. The closing segment grows to 60–90 min at HR 142–148.

Week 6's long carries the sub-9 gate: a flat 30 km at HR ≤148 holding ≤5:25/km, Nov 13, in the intended race shoe. Pass → the race plan is written for sub-9 with sub-10 as the net. Miss by >10 s/km → paced for a certain sub-10, with sub-9 only if the day allows.

Watch: shins, Friday closing pace trending down, gut on 90 g/h.
WEEK 7 · NOV 16–22

First taper step

Volume eases from the peak but the anchor doesn't: a 30 km Friday long carries the full dress-rehearsal fuelling card — race mix, cola from hour five, the table set up as on race day. Also the third of the three long runs a race shoe needs before Nov 13.

Feeds: the race-shoe short list, narrowing toward the Nov 13/20 calls.
WEEK 8 · NOV 23–29

Second taper step

45–55 km, long run down to 20 km. Kundo easy. The race plan itself is written around Nov 20, gated on the week-6 test: sub-9 pacing with sub-10 as the net, or a sub-10 pace with sub-9 only if the day allows.

Decides: final race shoe, fuelling-card starting rate (90 vs 75 g/h).
WEEK 9 · NOV 30–DEC 4

Race week

~15 km before the race, which lands on Friday — the normal long-run day. PRT 100 km, ~350 m loop, own table: stretch sub-9:00 (5:24/km), hard floor sub-10:00 (6:00/km), the pacing call locked in by the race plan.

Fuelling: 90 g/h at 6–8% dilution, solids/gel from hour one, cola from ~hour five.

After Dec 4

What comes after the loop.

No B-race follows this one — PRT 100 km closes the 2026 season. Recovery afterward follows the same pattern as every big effort this year: days off, then easy jogging back in, no fixed timeline until the body says otherwise. The next season-plan file starts from scratch once that's clear.

One note is already banked for 2027, not for this build: next summer and autumn should carry more hills and more threshold/tempo/speed, both crowded out by two ultra builds this year.
