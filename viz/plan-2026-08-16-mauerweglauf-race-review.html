—— Block 03 · week-by-week ——

The race is over.
Now the body writes the plan.

Berlin Mauerweglauf finished Saturday in 22:05:38 — 163.39 km through 34°C heat, a two-hour GI crash at km 98, and a cola-fuelled comeback that ran the final 60 km faster than the 20 before it. TRIMP 1583 is 3.3× the biggest session on record. This is recovery from an order-of-magnitude event, not a big long run, and the six weeks to Lidingöloppet are gated by what the body actually says, not by the calendar.

The taper's dominant open question — whether a compensating back would hold posture at hour 14 — resolved as cleanly as it could: zero left-side symptoms over 22 hours. What's sore now is new and unrelated: the right hip flexor, opposite side, ordinary 100-mile overuse alongside the expected blisters and loose toenails. It is the primary gate on resuming running — if still sore on brisk walking at D+7 (Aug 22), it gets a professional look before any jog.

Two watch items override the week-by-week table below. (1) The right hip flexor, as above. (2) Autonomic recovery — elevated easy-run heart rate in weeks 2–3 is expected after an event this size and should be read as information, not a fitness problem, the same pattern seen (milder) after Tuscany in May.

ASICS Superblast 3 is confirmed as the standing hot/flat ultra race shoe — 163 km with only cosmetic foot damage (blisters, loose toenails), not the platform fatigue the Aug 7 shoe switch was made to avoid. Strength drops to maintenance-only through the recovery block, per the standing post-race rule — heavy resumes with normal training.

Lidingöloppet participation is assumed from the season plan but worth a confirm at the next review — a September without a race is also an acceptable outcome of a finished A-race season.
