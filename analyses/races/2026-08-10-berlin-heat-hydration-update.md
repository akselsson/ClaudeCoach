# Berlin Mauerweglauf — heat amendment to the energiplan

Amends `races/2026-07-07-mauerweglauf-energiplan.md`. Everything in that file stands except where explicitly changed here. The race-day hip protocol in `schedule/2026-08-07-weekly-review.md` also stands, with one heat interaction noted at the end.

**Read in one line: the plan's "+35% fluid" hot column was written for a hot year, and this is worse than a hot year — the fix is not just drinking more, it is drinking *weaker*, because the fluid requirement roughly doubles in the middle of the race while the carb requirement falls, and running the existing 10% mix at the new fluid volume would put 90 g/h of carbohydrate into a gut that heat has already slowed down.**

## The forecast, checked

Patrik's 32–36°C is the majority read, but the model spread five days out is genuinely large and worth knowing:

| Model | Aug 15 max | Aug 15 min |
|---|---|---|
| **ECMWF IFS** | **36.8 °C** | 20.2 |
| UKMO | 32.4 | 22.1 |
| GFS | 32.9 | 20.7 |
| ICON (DWD) | 28.4 | 19.8 |

Three of four land in Patrik's 32–36 band; ICON is the low outlier. **Plan against ECMWF (the hot case).** Everything below that costs money or weight is cheap enough to carry unused; the reverse error is not recoverable mid-race.

**The single most useful fact: it is DRY heat.** Relative humidity at peak is 13% (ECMWF), 23% (GFS), 29% (UKMO), 36% (ICON) — every model agrees. Wet-bulb temperature in the ECMWF scenario is only ~19–20°C despite 36.8°C dry-bulb. Two consequences:

1. **Sweat evaporates efficiently, so cooling actually works.** Dousing, wetted sleeves and an iced bandana are far more effective at 36°C/13% RH than they would be at 30°C/80% RH. This is a survivable heat if it is managed, and the management has high leverage.
2. **He will not feel wet, so he will underestimate his losses.** In dry heat sweat evaporates off the skin before it beads. The subjective signal that normally says "you are sweating hard" is absent. Drink on the clock, not on feel.

Sunrise 05:49, sunset 20:32, UV index peaking ~5.7 — **14.5 hours of daylight, most of it on him.**

Three things fall out of this table:

- **Roughly seven hours above 34°C, spanning about km 63 to km 110.** That is 47 km in extreme heat, and it straddles both of the big drop bags.
- **The hardest single stretch is km 70 → km 102** — four hours between drop bags, at 35–37°C, arriving at halfway already 8 hours deep. That is where this race will be won or lost, and the only support in it is the VPs.
- **The night is not a rescue.** It is still 32°C at 21:00 and does not drop below 25°C before the finish. The plan's old framing — "heat is the day problem, night is the light problem" — no longer holds. There is no cool block in this race.

## The structural change: decouple fluid from carbs

This is the substantive amendment. Everything else is execution detail.

The energiplan's serving rule is **1× 90 g U Sport across both 450 ml flasks — 90 g per 900 ml, a ~10% mix.** That rule is correct at 20–25°C, and it was validated on Aug 9. In this heat it breaks, arithmetically:

- Fluid need in the peak block is ~950 ml/h.
- At 10% mix, 950 ml/h delivers **95 g CHO/h**.
- The energiplan's own target for km 70–102 is **68 g/h**, falling to 60 g/h.
- So holding the rehearsed mix at the required fluid volume means force-feeding ~40% more carbohydrate than planned into a gut whose blood supply heat has already cut. That is a direct route back to Jul 30.

**The fix: the mix concentration follows the temperature, not the clock.**

| Ambient | Mix | Delivers | Carb top-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **< 25 °C** (km 0–33, and after ~23:00) | **Full portion** — 90 g across both flasks (10%), exactly as rehearsed | 90 g / 900 ml | Gels as per plan |
| **> 30 °C** (km ~50 → km ~135, i.e. most of the race) | **Half portion** — 45 g across both flasks (5%) | 45 g / 900 ml | 1 gel/h or cola at VPs |
| 25–30 °C (the transitions) | Judgement — err weak | — | — |

The **45 g half-dose ziplocks are already in the energiplan** ("pre-make half-dose bags for the diluted late mix"). This amendment moves them from a late-race contingency to the main event: they now cover roughly km 50 to km 135. **Pack accordingly — the majority of the portions in the drop bags should be 45 g bags, not 90 g pouches.** Total carbohydrate over the race is essentially unchanged; only its concentration changes.

**A third flask is now mandatory, not optional.** The energiplan lists an "extra soft-flask for water on hot days." Do the carrying-capacity arithmetic: VP spacing runs 5–9 km, which at the peak-heat pace of ~7:30/km is 38–68 minutes between stations. At 950 ml/h a 68-minute gap needs **~1080 ml carried**, and 2×450 ml is 900 ml. He is short on the long gaps with zero margin for a slow section or a depleted VP. Carry three 450 ml flasks minimum through km 50–135; a fourth or a collapsible 500 ml is cheap insurance for the km 70→102 block specifically.

## Fluid budget

Replaces the "Dryck/h VARMT" column, which was built as a flat +35%. The real requirement is not flat — it is concentrated in the middle.

| Segment | Clock | Temp | Drink | Duration | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| km 0–33 | 06:00–09:30 | 20–26 | ~550 ml/h | 3.5 h | 1.9 L |
| km 33–70 | 09:30–13:45 | 26–35 | ~800 ml/h | 4.25 h | 3.4 L |
| **km 70–102** | 13:45–17:45 | **35–37** | **~950 ml/h** | 4.0 h | **3.8 L** |
| km 102–128 | 17:45–21:00 | 36–32 | ~800 ml/h | 3.25 h | 2.6 L |
| km 128–162 | 21:00–01:52 | 32–25 | ~600 ml/h | 4.85 h | 2.9 L |

**Total ≈ 14.5 L drunk**, against the energiplan's ~10 L for normal temperatures — about **+45%**, but with the increase loaded into the middle rather than spread evenly.

**Plus dousing water on top of this, and it does not come out of the drinking budget.** Budget roughly 500 ml per VP poured over head, neck and forearms — call it 8–10 L across the race, sourced entirely at stations. Ask for it separately and explicitly; do not ration drinking water in order to cool.

**~950 ml/h is at the ceiling of what a gut absorbs while running.** Do not chase a bigger number. In this heat the sweat rate will likely exceed 1.2 L/h during the peak block, meaning **a fluid deficit is unavoidable and must be accepted.** The way to shrink it is not to drink more than the gut can take — it is to run slower and to cool the skin. See pacing below.

## Sodium — now the safety item, not a detail

Halving the mix halves the sodium that comes with it. 45 g of U Sport carries ~375 mg Na; at 950 ml/h that is ~395 mg/h against a hot-weather target of **700–1000 mg/h**. The gap has to be filled deliberately.

- **From ~km 40 to ~km 135: 1× U Hydrate (500 mg) per hour**, dissolved in the plain-water flask rather than the carb flask.
- **If cramping, or if drinking above 900 ml/h: add 2× Saltstick FastChews (200 mg) per 45 min**, and U Salty on top if it persists.
- **Count the tablets before packing.** The >28°C window runs roughly 10:30 to 22:30 — about **12 hours, so 12+ U Hydrate**, plus FastChews. Split across all three drop bags with genuine redundancy; "stock extra" is not a number. Carry 3–4 on the person from the start, because the first heat arrives before the km 33 bag.
- **This is the hyponatremia guard.** Drinking ~14 L over 20 hours with insufficient sodium is the classic way to end up in a medical tent at a hot 100-miler. Symptoms to catch early: sloshing stomach, puffy fingers, ring or watch strap tightening, nausea with clear and copious urine. **If those appear, the answer is more salt and less water — not more water.**

## Ice, thermoses and the bandana — what will actually work

The idea is sound and the low humidity makes it better than it would be in a humid heat. But it needs a reality check on melt, and a correction on what ice is actually for.

### Reality check on the thermoses

Ice survival at 36°C ambient depends almost entirely on total ice mass, headspace, and whether the bag sits in the sun. Working estimate, assuming bags are dropped race morning:

| Bag | Reaches it at | Elapsed from drop | Realistic state |
|---|---|---|---|
| km 33 | ~09:30 | ~4.5 h | **Mostly solid ice.** The plan works here. |
| km 70 | ~13:45 | ~9 h | **Partial — expect roughly half melted.** Still a genuine win. |
| km 102 | ~17:45 | ~13 h | **Do not count on ice.** Plan it as cold water, treat slush as a bonus. |

**The one thing that could break this entirely: if drop bags must be handed in on Friday evening rather than Saturday morning, add ~10 hours to every row and the km 70 and km 102 thermoses arrive as warm water.** This is a hard dependency and it is currently unverified — **check the drop-bag hand-in time before freezing anything.** If it is Friday, the ice plan survives only at km 33, and the rest has to come from VPs or from a supporter.

### Making the ice last

- **Wide-mouth vacuum flask.** The most common failure here is a narrow-neck thermos containing a block of ice that cannot be got out. If it will not take a hand or a scoop, it is the wrong container.
- **Large cubes packed tight, not small cubes, and not a single frozen block.** Big cubes minimise surface area (slow melt) while staying retrievable. A solid block wins on melt rate and loses on being unusable.
- **Pre-chill the flask** — fill with ice water for 30 minutes, dump it, then fill with ice. A room-temperature flask spends its first hour melting ice just to cool its own walls.
- **Fill completely, no water, no headspace.** Air is what melts ice.
- **Insulate inside the drop bag:** wrap the flask in a foil emergency blanket and pack clothing around it. Ask for the bags to be kept in shade if there is anyone to ask.

### What ice is for, in priority order

1. **Into the softflasks.** Best core-temperature return per gram, and — the underrated part — at 36°C the flasks reach ambient within half an hour, and warm 5% U Sport is genuinely hard to keep drinking. Cold fluid is also emptied from the stomach faster than warm. **The main risk to hydration in this race is palatability, and ice is the direct fix.**
2. **The bandana round the neck.** Do it — but with honest expectations: at 36°C it is roughly a **10–15 minute** effect, and the neck is more a perceptual cooling site than a thermally powerful one. Its real value is that feeling cooler holds pace discipline together, which is not nothing.
3. **A handful in the cap, and a cube in each palm.** Palms are glabrous skin and are a genuinely effective heat exchanger — better physiology than the neck, less pleasant.

### The correction worth making

**Ice is available at three points on the course. Water is available at twenty-six.** In 13–30% humidity, a *wetted* bandana is nearly as effective as an iced one once the first fifteen minutes have passed, because evaporation — not conduction — is doing most of the work.

So the primary cooling strategy is **rewet everything at every single VP**: bandana, cap, sleeves, shirt. The ice at km 33 / 70 / 102 is a bonus layer on top of that, not the strategy itself. Building the plan around three thermoses and neglecting twenty-six water points would be backwards.

**Pour on head, neck, forearms and shirt — not down the legs.** Water into the shoes over 160 km is a blister problem, and blisters are already a live concern with a shoe that has 41 km on it.

### Clothing

- **Light, loose, long-sleeved or white sun sleeves — wettable.** In dry heat a wetted sleeve outperforms bare skin, and bare skin under UV 5–6 for fourteen hours means sunburn, which itself impairs thermoregulation for the rest of the race.
- **Cap with a neck flap**, kept wet.
- **Sunscreen before the start, reapplied at km 33 and km 70.** Put it in those bags now.

## Pacing — because pace is a hydration variable

Metabolic heat production scales with running speed. In a race where the fluid deficit is unavoidable, **slowing down is the only lever that reduces the amount of sweat that needs replacing.** It belongs in the hydration plan, not somewhere else.

- **Sub-20h is off in the hot scenario.** The 19:52 scaffold in the energiplan was always explicitly a device for deriving g/h, and in 36°C it is fiction. Expect **21–23 hours**. The 30h cutoff is not remotely in play, so there is nothing to defend.
- **Do not bank time in the cool morning.** Every minute banked between 06:00 and 09:30 is repaid with interest at 15:30, because arriving at the peak-heat block with a higher core temperature is precisely the failure mode. This is the same error that ended the Jul 30 50 km at 50.5 km, and it is the single most likely way this race goes wrong.
- **Actively slow into the peak block.** From ~km 60, add 30–45 s/km relative to what feels sustainable. It will feel like giving up ground. It is buying the second half.
- **Walk more, earlier, in the heat.** This already sits in the hip protocol for posture reasons; the heat gives it a second independent justification.

## GI — the Jul 30 failure mode, one degree closer

Heat diverts blood from the gut, so gastric emptying slows exactly when intake needs to rise. Diluting the mix is the primary defence and is why this amendment exists. Second-line, unchanged from the energiplan and reaffirmed:

**If the gut goes quiet: stop the carbs for 30–45 minutes.** Plain water, salt, and cola only, dropping to ~30 g/h, then rebuild slowly. *Lower g/h briefly beats shutting the gut down* — and the cost of getting this wrong was demonstrated on Jul 30.

Take cola at VPs freely from km 70 on. It is usually the coldest thing on the table, it is carbohydrate that requires no mixing, and palatability is a real constraint by hour ten.

## Caffeine

One small change. The energiplan holds caffeine until ~km 96, which now lands at ~17:00 — inside the hottest hours. Caffeine is mildly thermogenic and the diuretic effect, while overstated in general, is not helpful at a 1.2 L/h sweat rate.

**Shift the first dose to the km 102 drop bag (~17:45), once the temperature has started falling.** The rest of the staging is unchanged; the U Intend 250 mg one-shot for km 148–162 still makes sense and now sits in genuinely cooling air.

## Heat illness — DNF criteria

The standing rule is DNF over injury. This adds a category with a faster clock, because heat stroke is the failure mode at a hot 100-miler that does not give a long warning and does not resolve with grit.

**Stop at a VP, get into shade, take ice and cold fluid, and do not restart until it clears:**

- **Any CNS symptom — confusion, disorientation, unusual irritability, stumbling or unsteady gait, difficulty following a conversation.** This is the discriminator. Ordinary ultra fatigue does not affect cognition; heat illness does, and it does so before the runner notices.
- **Sweating stops, or chills / goosebumps appear while it is still hot.**
- **Headache with nausea and no other explanation.**
- **No urination for 4+ hours, or very dark urine.**

The awkward part is that early heat exhaustion looks a great deal like hour 12 of a 100-miler. **The rule that resolves it: if anyone at a VP asks whether he is alright, take it as data rather than as fussing.** Losing 20 minutes in shade is recoverable. The alternative is not.

## Heat acclimation — Aug 10–13, and it has to start today

Five days is not full acclimation, but **plasma volume expansion is the fastest adaptation (3–5 days) and it is exactly the one that helps here.** This window closes tomorrow, so it is the most time-sensitive item in this file.

**Passive heat only — sauna or hot bath. No overdressed running.** The hip/back has responded to rest and race week is already rest-weighted; passive heat delivers most of the adaptation with essentially zero mechanical load, which is the right trade given the referred-symptom situation.

| Day | Protocol |
|---|---|
| **Mon Aug 10** | Sauna 25–30 min (or 40°C bath, 30–40 min). Standalone. |
| **Tue Aug 11** | **Immediately after** the easy 8 km — post-exercise is the highest-yield version. 25–30 min. |
| **Wed Aug 12** | Sauna 25–30 min, standalone. |
| **Thu Aug 13** | After the shakeout. 20–25 min. |
| **Fri Aug 14** | **None.** Travel day. Prioritise sleep and hydration. |

Rehydrate fully after each session — the point is to trigger the adaptation, not to arrive at the start line down a litre. This does not replace anything in the race-week schedule; it is bolted onto sessions that were already prescribed.

**Final 48 hours:** normal fluids plus generous salt on food Thursday and Friday. No glycerol or sodium-hyperhydration protocols — untested, and race week is not for experiments. Race morning: ~500 ml with electrolyte 90 minutes before the start, then sip.

## What changes from the energiplan, and why

- **The hot-weather protocol is no longer conditional.** It was written to trigger at a km 45 heat-check. Under this forecast it is **on from the gun**, and the **km 45 check inverts into a de-escalation check** — if ICON verifies and it tops out at 28°C, step back toward the normal column. Planning to trigger a protocol at km 45 in a race that is 26°C at km 33 is planning to be late.
- **The "+35% fluid" hot column is replaced by a segment budget totalling ~14.5 L (+45%)**, loaded into km 70–102 rather than spread flat. A flat uplift under-serves the peak block and over-serves the morning.
- **The serving rule now varies with temperature.** 90 g/900 ml below 25°C, **45 g/900 ml above 30°C** — covering roughly km 50–135, i.e. most of the race. This is the biggest single change and it exists because fluid need and carb need move in opposite directions in heat. Total carbohydrate is unchanged; concentration is not. Pack mostly 45 g bags.
- **The third soft-flask moves from optional to mandatory**, on carrying-capacity arithmetic: 900 ml does not cover a 68-minute VP gap at 950 ml/h.
- **Sodium gets a count rather than an instruction** — 12+ U Hydrate plus FastChews, 1/h from km 40 to km 135, in the water flask. Halving the mix halved the sodium that used to arrive with it.
- **Ice is added, and demoted.** Thermoses at km 33 and km 70 are worth doing and will largely work; **km 102 should be planned as cold water, not ice.** The primary cooling strategy is rewetting at all 26 VPs — 26 water points beat 3 thermoses. Ice's best use is in the flasks, for palatability and gastric emptying, ahead of the neck bandana.
- **First caffeine moves from ~km 96 to the km 102 bag**, out of the hottest hours.
- **Heat-illness DNF criteria are added**, with CNS symptoms as the discriminator. Distinct from the neuro red flags in the hip protocol, which remain in force separately.
- **Pacing is explicitly part of the hydration plan.** Sub-20h is treated as gone in the hot scenario; the instruction not to bank time in the cool morning is the highest-value line in this file.
- **Four passive heat-acclimation sessions Aug 10–13** are added onto the existing race-week schedule. Nothing in that schedule is removed or changed.

**Interaction with the hip protocol:** heat brings general fatigue forward, and general fatigue is what precedes the trunk collapse the whole hip protocol is built around. So the posture check-ins and the deliberate early walk breaks are not merely still valid — they arrive **earlier** than they would on a cool day. **The km 70 reassessment (~13:45, ~35°C, halfway, eight hours in) is now the genuine decision point of the race**, and it should be treated as one rather than as a drop-bag stop.
