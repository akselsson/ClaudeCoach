# Berlin Mauerweglauf — hydration & cooling execution card

**Race:** Sat 2026-08-15, start 06:00, 162.1 km, 26 VPs, 3 drop bags (km 33 / km 70 / km 102).
**Forecast:** 32–36 °C, sunny, **dry** (RH 13–35%). Sunrise 05:49, sunset 20:32.

This is the operating document. It supersedes the hydration sections of `races/2026-07-07-mauerweglauf-energiplan.md` and consolidates `races/2026-08-10-berlin-heat-hydration-update.md`, which remains the place to look for *why*. The energiplan's non-hydration content and the race-day hip protocol in `schedule/2026-08-07-weekly-review.md` are unchanged and still apply.

**The three rules that matter most, if everything else falls out of your head:**

1. **Don't bank time in the cool morning.** Every minute gained before 09:30 is repaid with interest at 15:30.
2. **Full-strength mix throughout — add water alongside it, never into it.** Two sips of mix to one of water once it's over 32 °C.
3. **Water on the outside at every single VP.** Twenty-six water points beat three thermoses.

## Revision note — why this differs from the amendment

The heat amendment said "half-mix above 30 °C, make up the carbs with gels." **That rule was over-applied and this card replaces it.**

The required concentration is simply **carb target ÷ fluid rate**, and it varies by segment far more than the amendment allowed:

| Segment | Carb target | Fluid | Required concentration |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · km 0–33 | ~88 g/h | 750 ml/h | **~11%** → full strength |
| B · km 33–70 | ~78 g/h | 800 ml/h | **~9.8%** → full strength |
| **C · km 70–102** | ~68 g/h | 950 ml/h | **~7.2%** → needs water |
| D · km 102–128 | ~65 g/h | 800 ml/h | **~8.1%** → needs a little water |
| E · km 128–162 | ~60 g/h | 600 ml/h | **~10%** → full strength |

**Only the crux block genuinely requires thinning.** Blanket-halving from km 33 onward over-diluted three segments out of five and manufactured a gel problem that didn't need to exist.

**And the thinning is better done in the drinker than in the powder.** 680 ml of 10% mix plus 270 ml of plain water is 68 g in 950 ml — arithmetically identical to a pre-diluted 7.2% flask, but it keeps three advantages:

- **The rehearsed action survives.** One 90 g portion emptied across both flasks, exactly as validated on Aug 9. No new portion sizes, no ratio arithmetic at hour ten in the sun.
- **The carb-free escape hatch stays live.** With diluted mix in both flasks every sip carries carbohydrate. With a dedicated plain-water flask you can go to zero carbs instantly if the gut protests — the same lever, applied reactively instead of preemptively.
- **You control the ratio continuously**, rather than committing to it at a drop bag 35 km earlier.

**The honest risk this carries:** 10% mix in real heat is untested for you. Aug 9 proved 10% at ~20 °C over 98 minutes. Jul 30 disproved ~20% in heat. 10% at 36 °C sits between a proven point and a disproven one, and U Sport is formulated for roughly that concentration — but it is an assumption, not a finding. **The water flask is the mitigation. If the gut goes quiet in block C, shift the ratio toward water immediately rather than waiting to see.**

## The operating table

Anchored to the energiplan's 19:52 scaffold. **If you are an hour down on it, shift each row an hour later** — the two hot rows barely move, the night rows arrive cooler. One more reason not to chase the scaffold.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Clock** | 06:00–09:30 | 09:30–13:45 | **13:45–17:45** | 17:45–21:00 | 21:00–01:52 |

| **Of which mix** | 750 ml/h | 800 → 530 ml/h | **680 ml/h** | 650 ml/h | 600 ml/h |
| **Carbs** | ~75 g/h | ~70 g/h | **~68 g/h** | ~65 g/h | ~60 g/h |

| **Salt** | mix only (~625 mg/h) | 2 FastChews at VP 6 and VP 8 | **alternate at every VP: tablet, chews, tablet…** | same alternation to VP 20 | tablet at VP 21 and VP 24 |

| **Segment total** | ~2.6 L | ~2.9 L | **~3.8 L** | ~2.9 L | ~3.2 L |

**Total drunk ≈ 15.4 L — 12.8 L of mix and 2.6 L of plain water alongside it.** Plus roughly **8–10 L poured over yourself**, sourced entirely at VPs; that water is not part of the drinking budget. Ask for it separately. Carbohydrate from drink alone comes to **1283 g, averaging 65 g/h.**

**Segment A drinks more than thirst asks for — that is deliberate.** 750 ml/h at 20–26 °C is above what you would take naturally, and it is the cheapest fluid of the day. You are pre-loading for block C, and the sodium arrives with it.

**Start the 2:1 ratio at VP 9 / Falkenseer Chaussee (km 51.0, ~11:33, 32 °C)** — not at the km 70 bag. It's already 33 °C well before Sacrow, and easing the concentration down before the crux beats stepping it down at the moment the crux begins.

**Three flasks is enough for every leg on the real course.** With the official VP positions the longest carry of the whole race is **VP 19 → VP 20, 8.7 km / 870 ml — 64% of 1350 ml.** The tightest carry is in block D, not block C as previously assumed. A fourth flask is worthwhile margin against a dry VP or a slower-than-planned leg; it is not a requirement.

**950 ml/h is the ceiling of what a gut absorbs while running, and sweat rate in block C will exceed it. The deficit is unavoidable — accept it.** The way to shrink it is to run slower and cool the skin, not to drink more.

**Cola now displaces mix, it does not supplement it.** Take it when it's cold and you want it — but if you take a lot of cola, take correspondingly less mix, or you'll overshoot the carb target in exactly the block where that matters.

## The VP routine

Twenty-six stations. Same six things, every time, sixty seconds:

1. **Fill** — mix flasks and the water flask, both. Fresh 90 g portion when the mix runs out.
2. **Douse** — head, neck, forearms, shirt. **Never down the legs** — water in the shoes over 160 km is a blister problem.
3. **Rewet** — bandana, cap, sleeves.
4. **Salt** — from VP 9 to VP 20, **alternate: U Hydrate at the odd VPs, 2 FastChews at the even ones.**
5. **Posture** — stand tall, hips forward, reset. *(The hip protocol's check-in — same cadence, same stop.)*
6. **Cola** — from km 50 on, if you want it. Coldest thing on the table.

**Salt is pinned to the stations, not to the clock.** Legs run 30–65 minutes, so an hourly cadence drifts off the VPs and silently under-doses. Alternating tablet/chews at every VP through the hot block averages ~430 mg/h on top of the ~450–510 mg/h the mix already carries — mid-band for 32–37 °C, and it needs no timer.

## If the gut goes quiet

Heat diverts blood away from the gut exactly when intake has to rise — the Jul 30 failure mode, one degree closer. With full-strength mix this is the risk you are carrying deliberately, so the response has to be quick rather than clever. **Escalate in this order:**

1. **Shift the ratio** — go to 1:1 mix:water, then 1:2. This alone drops you from 10% to 5% without changing anything you're carrying. *First response, do it early.*
2. **Water and salt only for 20 minutes.** Carbs to zero. The plain-water flask exists for exactly this.
3. **Rebuild at ~30 g/h** — cola and one gel — then work back up to the mix.

*Lower g/h briefly beats shutting the gut down.* Do not wait for nausea to become certainty; the cost of easing off for twenty minutes is trivial and the cost of Jul 30 was 110 km.

## Thermos technique

- **Wide-mouth flasks only.** A narrow neck holding a block you can't extract is the classic failure.
- **Pre-chill each one** — fill with ice water for 30 min, dump, then pack.
- **Large cubes, packed tight, to the brim, no added water.** Big cubes minimise surface area while staying retrievable; air is what melts ice.
- **Wrap in a foil emergency blanket** and pack clothing around it. Ask for the bags to be kept in shade.

**What the ice is for, in priority order:** into the flasks first — best core-temperature return per gram, and **warm 10% U Sport at hour ten is a genuine palatability problem**, which now matters more than it did, because the mix is carrying the whole plan. Then the neck bandana, honestly a 10–15 minute effect at 36 °C. Then a handful in the cap and a cube in each palm.

## Decision gates

| Where | When | What you are deciding |
|---|---|---|
| **km 45** | ~11:00 | **De-escalation check.** The hot protocol is already running. If it is clearly not going above 30 °C, hold mix-only through the whole race and drop the water ratio entirely. |
| **km 55** | ~12:00 | **Ratio step-down.** 32 °C. Go to 2 mix : 1 water. Also the first honest read on whether 10% is sitting well in heat. |
| **km 70** | ~13:45 | **The real decision point of the race.** Halfway, eight hours in, 35 °C. Hip and posture, GI, urine colour, whether you are cooling successfully. Take the time — it's a bag stop anyway. |
| **km 102** | ~17:45 | Night gear on, first caffeine, second formal reassessment. Last resupply — check you are carrying enough for 60 km. |

**Urine check at each bag.** Pale straw is the target. Dark and scant means behind on fluid. Clear, copious *and* nauseous means behind on **salt** — the answer is a tablet, not another bottle.

## Red flags

### Heat illness — stop, shade, ice, cold fluid. Do not restart until it clears.

- **Any CNS symptom: confusion, disorientation, unusual irritability, stumbling, difficulty following a conversation.** ← **this is the discriminator.** Ordinary hour-12 fatigue does not touch cognition; heat illness does, before you notice.
- Sweating stops, or chills/goosebumps while it is still hot
- Headache with nausea and no other explanation
- No urination for 4+ hours, or very dark urine

**If anyone at a VP asks whether you're alright, treat it as data, not fussing.** Twenty minutes in shade is recoverable.

### Hyponatremia — more salt, less water

Sloshing stomach · puffy fingers · ring or watch strap tightening · nausea with clear copious urine.

### Neuro / back — DNF criteria, unchanged

Numbness or pins-and-needles down the leg · loss of strength (knee buckling, foot slap or drag) · symptoms going bilateral · any bowel or bladder change. *Per `schedule/2026-08-07-weekly-review.md`. Ordinary aching and the familiar hip/knee grumble are not on this list.*

**DNF over injury, every time.** Lidingöloppet is Sep 26 and a disc is a multi-season problem.
