# Mauerweglauf 2026 — race review

**Result: 163.39 km in 22:05:38 (moving 18:55:49), ~34°C, finisher.** Goal was sub-20; the miss decomposes into the heat (~half) and one GI crash (~90–120 min). Full numeric record and pacing table: `syncs/2026-08-15-mauerweglauf-race-sync.md`. This file is the interpretation and the forward plan. Debrief with Patrik 2026-08-16.

**Read in one line: the plan's machinery — HR ceiling, cooling, the gut-crash escalation ladder, the hip protocol — all worked; what failed was hydration volume through the crux block, and it announced itself 18 km before the crash.**

## The crash — causal autopsy

Three suspects were named in the Strava description: the old gel, drinking too much, the heat. The debrief moves the weight decisively toward a fluid-deficit chain:

1. **The gel is (mostly) exonerated — and only ~20% of it was consumed.** It was a Maurten, on plan, but it *tasted stale* and Patrik threw it out after roughly a fifth (clarified 2026-08-16). Two consequences: (a) ~5 g of even a degraded gel is an implausible sole cause of a 2 h shutdown, which shifts more weight onto the fluid chain below; (b) the stale taste means "gammal" may have been genuinely degraded product, not just old stock — so the discard was exactly right, and there *is* a small hygiene lesson after all: **check gel dates when packing drop bags, and keep trusting the taste veto** — it worked.
2. **The felt water shortage at ~km 80 is the real finding.** Patrik felt under-watered around km 80 and drank a large volume at the km 80 aid station. Crash at km 98–103. That sequence is textbook: a hot-gut fluid deficit accumulates → gastric emptying is already impaired → a large cold bolus sits and sloshes → the next gel tips it over. The plan *predicted* this exact deficit — the Aug 11 revision states plainly that in block C "sweat rate exceeds what the gut absorbs — the deficit is unavoidable." What the plan did not do was flag **felt thirst at km 80 as a red alert**. It should have: on the early-warning ladder (taste → reluctance → sloshing → burping → nausea), genuine thirst-plus-bolus-drinking is a lateral entry at about rung 3.
3. **Heat is the background condition, not an explanation.** 34°C made everything tighter, but the cooling protocol was holding (Patrik's own read: navigating the heat "helt ok" until km 98).

**Lesson of record for the next hot ultra:** the response to feeling short of water at an aid station is *not* one large bolus — it is smaller volumes at the next several VPs plus an immediate concentration cut (the half-flask lever), treating the thirst itself as the "act at 1 or 2" signal the ladder already prescribes. Drink ahead of thirst in the crux block; the sheet's per-leg ml were right, the delivery got behind.

## The escalation ladder — validated end to end

This is the review's best news after the hip. The Aug 11 revision amended the recovery estimate from the card's 20 minutes to **"one to two hours, budget for the longer version"** — based on Patrik's prior race history. The race then ran the experiment: ~2 h of nausea waited out at the km 103 VP (84 min stopped), water and time, then a rebuild on cola at roughly the ladder's prescribed ~30 g/h.

The rebuild did not merely salvage a finish. **Km 121–140 ran at 7:00/km moving — faster than km 81–100 pre-crash — and the final 3 km at 7:17/km.** Sixty kilometres on a "coca cola-diet" with a negative-splitting back end. Two durable conclusions:

- **The ladder is now race-proven as written** (shift to water → water+electrolytes for 1–2 h → rebuild at ~30 g/h). Do not re-litigate it in future plans; cite it.
- **The engine was never the limiter.** HR discipline (87% below 140, nothing above 150) left enough aerobic reserve at km 120+ to *speed up*. The pacing philosophy — treat the HR cap as non-negotiable and let pace float — is validated for this distance.

## The hip — the build's biggest question, answered

**No problems. At all. Over 22 hours.** The left hip/knee complex that cost five days of the taper, produced a working lumbar-disc theory, a race-day protocol with km 70/km 102 reassessment gates, and a durable-pain stop rule — never triggered anything. The km 71 dwell (14 min) doubled as the planned gate and passed. The naprapat's "cost, not damage" framing is now backed by the hardest possible test.

What this does *not* mean: that the disc theory was wrong or the concern wasted. It means load at HR 128 on a flat course was inside tolerance. The descent-penalty series and the back-compensation question stay open as a *training* question, not a race risk. **Worth one follow-up naprapat visit** with the new data point: 163 km, zero left-side symptoms.

**New and separate: the RIGHT hip flexor is sore post-race.** Opposite side, classic 100-mile overuse territory alongside the blisters and loose toenails Patrik correctly files under "as expected." Watch, don't treat, for a few days — but it is the primary gate on resuming running (below).

## Cooling — verdict and a crew note

As good as solo logistics allow: ice from the thermos at the three drop bags, water dousing elsewhere. Empirically **ice lasts ~5 km** in that heat. For any future crewed hot race, the instruction writes itself: crew re-ices every ~5 km. Solo, the only improvements are marginal (ice at whichever VPs stock it — ask at briefing, which remained unanswered this year). The thermos technique itself is proven and keeps its place in the playbook.

## Superblast 3 — race-shoe verdict

Carried 163 km with foot damage limited to blisters and toenails — i.e. the expected 100-mile toll, not the Evo SL-style platform fatigue the Aug 7 shoe switch was made to avoid. The size-up-for-swell logic and the stability rationale both held. The Evo SL backup at km 33 was not needed. **Superblast 3 is the standing hot/flat ultra race shoe until contradicted.**

## Lessons of record (for the next 100-miler)

1. **Thirst in the crux block is a rung-3 alarm, not a supply request.** Respond with smaller repeated volumes + concentration cut, never one bolus.
2. **The gut-crash ladder works and takes 1–2 h; budget it in the goal math.** A hot-day 100-mile goal should carry a ~90 min contingency explicitly.
3. **Checkpoint dwell is a first-class time budget.** ~1:45 of non-crash dwell across 26 VPs; sub-X plans must price it (the sub-20 scaffold didn't, honestly).
4. **HR-capped pacing at ~128 avg leaves the engine alive at km 140.** Keep the cap sacred; the pace takes care of itself.
5. **Ice lasts 5 km.** Crewed hot races: re-ice at that cadence.
6. **Stability platform > racy platform for 100 miles.** Feet came out with only cosmetic damage.
