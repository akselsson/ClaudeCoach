# Lidingöloppet 2026 — race review

**Result: 30.34 km in 2:27:15, avg HR 169.3, max 184, in the Metaspeed Sky Paris.** 2025 was 2:24:07 at avg HR 168.3 in the Boston 13. Numeric record, zone breakdown and load in `../syncs/2026-09-26-lidingoloppet-race-sync.md`. This file is the interpretation. Debrief with Patrik same day.

Patrik's Strava note: *"Trångt i början, kändes bra i mitten, ingen energi i benen på slutet."* His question was where the 3 minutes went, with the observation that Strava did not show him as much slower at the end as it had felt. **That observation is correct, and the review is built around it.**

## Where the 3:08 went

Both traces aligned on distance run, with grade-adjusted pace (GAP) from a 50 m smoothed altitude stream and walking detected at <2.0 m/s. Same method as `2026-09-19-lidingoloppet-course-model.md`.

| Section | 2025 | 2026 | Lost | Running total | Avg HR 2025 → 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| km 0–3 | 13:14 | 14:36 | **+1:22** | +1:22 | 160 → 151 |
| km 3–10 | 30:56 | 31:47 | +0:51 | +2:13 | 166 → 164 |
| km 10–17 | 32:27 | 32:29 | +0:02 | +2:15 | 167 → 169 |
| km 17–20 | 14:49 | 15:16 | +0:27 | +2:42 | 170 → 174 |
| km 20–28 | 39:32 | 39:39 | +0:07 | +2:49 | 170 → 175 |
| km 29 | 5:38 | 6:15 | **+0:37** | +3:26 | 173 → 176 |
| km 30 + finish | 6:37 | 6:20 | −0:17 | +3:09 | 175 → 180 |

Four separate stories, in order of size:

### 1. The congested start — ~1:22, not effort

Heart rate over the first 3 km sat 10 bpm below 2025 over identical ground, and it took 6:07 to first reach 160 against 3:07 last year. GAP was ~4:45 where 2025 ran ~4:18, at a heart rate he can hold for hours. That is a slower start group or a worse position in it, not fitness and not pacing. The start time was also 10 minutes earlier than 2025 (12:30 vs 12:40), which is consistent with a different group. **Nothing to fix in training; a seeding question for next year.**

### 2. The cautious opening — ~0:51, by design

Km 3–10 ran 2–4 bpm under 2025, exactly as the staged cap (≤163 to km 13) prescribed. He executed the plan. The plan's bet was that this would buy a faster back half than 2025. See §4 for the verdict on that bet.

### 3. Km 10–28 was a dead heat, at a higher price

Over 18 km the two races differ by 9 seconds. The hike list was executed: walking at km 20.8, km 24.6, Abborrbacken (km 25.2) and the km 28.4 climb, in both years, at nearly identical durations. Between the climbs the running was, if anything, slightly faster this year.

But from km 17 onward heart rate ran **4–5 bpm higher for the same pace**, and grade-adjusted metres per beat were ~5% lower than 2025 over the same stretch. Whole-race efficiency: 1.286 → 1.246 m/beat (−3%). He held 2025's speed by spending more. That is the cost of a 163 km ultra six weeks earlier, an illness week on Sep 7, and four alcohol-compromised nights in race week — the race-week file predicted "the cap will simply deliver less", and it did. **This is a fitness observation, not a pacing failure.**

### 4. Km 29 — 0:37, and it was a decision

Walking on the final climb lasted 122 s against 76 s in 2025, and there was a second 19 s walk at km 28.95 on a **3% grade** after the top. Every other walk in either year was on 6–13%. Then km 30 was 10 s *faster* than 2025 at 179–182 bpm, finishing at a new max of 184.

Patrik's own read: *"I had mostly given up on getting a good time by 29 km, I'm pretty sure I could have run faster there if I had anything to run for."* The data agrees on all three counts — the near-flat walk, the fast last km, the max-HR finish. Legs that were empty at km 29 do not produce that km 30. **The "ingen energi i benen" was real as a feeling, but the km 29 minute was motivational, not physiological.**

## The plan's bet, honestly scored

The course model's expected shape was "slower through km 10, level by km 20, faster over the closing 10 km." What happened: slower through km 10 (mostly the crowd), level to km 28, equal on the close. The negative-split signature from the Ursvik head-to-head did not reproduce here.

Two conclusions, both worth carrying:

- **The staged HR cap is not the lever that beats 2025.** Executed as written, it produced an equal race from km 10 on, at higher heart rate. It is a sound *protection* strategy — nothing blew up, the km 20–25 fade the model located in 2025 did not recur — but it does not manufacture time. Fitness does. Patrik's own note says the same thing: *"till nästa år ska träna mer backe."*
- **Strip out the crowd and the km 29 surrender and this was a ~1:30 race against last year**, on a body carrying an ultra and an illness. That is a fairer comparison than 3:08, and a fair result for a B-race run "on whatever fitness remains", which is what the season plan asked for.

## Lesson of record: a B-race still needs something to run for

The race-week file said "no target time", on purpose, to keep the HR discipline honest. The cost surfaced at km 28: already well outside 2025, no fallback goal, no reason to hurt for two more kilometres.

**Rule going forward:** every race, including B-races run to a cap, carries a fallback target that survives a bad start. It can be a process target rather than a clock one — "km 25 to the finish faster than last year", "run every climb under X m", "no walking on anything under 6%". It has to be something that is still achievable at km 28 of a bad day, because that is exactly when it is needed. The cap protects the race; the fallback finishes it.

## Shoe

No measurable Metaspeed advantage over the Boston 13 at equal effort: whole-race GAP 4:45 at HR 169.3 against 4:37 at HR 168.3 last year. The shoe-chart estimate was −29 s/km with a stated confound (he wears it on good days); today was not a good day, and the fitness deficit swamps any shoe effect. **This race is not evidence against the Metaspeed** — it is one confounded point — but the "free minutes" the race-week file hoped for were not visible. Keep the shoe-chart verdict, add this run to it, and let the next chart rebuild weigh it.

## Damage and recovery

No niggles reported in the debrief. Quads were a watch item after Thursday's session; no report either way from the race. Treat this as a hard 30 km race at 91% of max HR for 2:27, i.e. a heavier day than anything since Mauerweglauf, and recover like it.
