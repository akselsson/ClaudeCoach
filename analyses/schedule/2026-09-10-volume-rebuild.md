## What changed, and why the Sep 7 file was wrong in shape

Gate 1 passed (`../syncs/2026-09-10-activity-sync.md`): no fever, no chest symptoms, no niggle, resting HR 46 against a baseline of 44, and 50 minutes to HR 170 tolerated cleanly. **The illness chapter is closed.**

Patrik's read, which is correct and is the load-bearing insight of this file: *"I have hardly run the last few weeks. My resting HR is never as low as 44 unless I'm in a higher volume period. That also goes for the running HR. So I'm fairly certain that what I need now is more running not less."*

Both post-illness signals — resting HR 2 bpm high, and ~5 bpm elevated at matched grade-adjusted pace — are fully explained by plasma-volume and stroke-volume loss across four weeks of near-zero running. They were being read as illness residue. They are a training-state readout, and they respond to volume.

### The structural error being corrected

Worth recording plainly, because it is a pattern and not a one-off.

The Sep 7 file set a target of **~40–50 km** for Sep 7–13 and **~45–55 km** for Sep 14–20. Inside those weeks it kept:

- a **23 km Ursvik trail run with 500 m+ of vert** under a 2:15 clock, and
- an **18–20 km run with 8–10 km continuous at 4:36–4:45/km**.

That is a peak-week session inside a re-entry week. When the plan came under pressure it deleted easy kilometres — the cheap, safe, adaptive part — and preserved the expensive sessions, because those are the ones with a stated "purpose." The hard-to-easy ratio ratcheted upward at exactly the moment Patrik was least equipped to absorb it.

The same reflex nearly repeated on Sep 10: the first response to the gate run was to propose moving Ursvik out — cutting volume again — while leaving the Sep 18 decision workout untouched.

**Planning rule going forward: when a week has to shrink, the hard session shrinks first and the easy volume is the last thing cut.** A demanding session is only affordable in proportion to the aerobic week around it. This applies to future blocks, not just this one.

Historical context for the same bias: prescribed ~75 km (May 25) → ran 72; prescribed ~78–80 km (Jun 1) → ran 68; prescribed ~78–80 base (Jul 20) → **ran 96**; B2B block (Jul 13) → **ran 109**. In the build Patrik ran above prescription twice by 20–30 km. The plans have consistently trailed his appetite for easy volume while never trimming the hard days.

### Fri Sep 18 is demoted from gate to rehearsal

The Sep 7 file made this a go/no-go: hold 4:36–4:40/km at HR ≤166 → race sub-2:20; cost HR 170+ → race the working target; fail → race the floor.

Drop that framing. **The test can no longer measure the thing it was built to measure.** On Sep 18 Patrik will be ~10 days into a volume rebuild from a four-week hole; on Sep 26 he will have had two more weeks of volume plus a taper. HR-at-pace is depressed by exactly the detraining that is being reversed, so a Sep 18 reading will *systematically under-read* race-day fitness — and would push the goal down for a reason that will no longer be true on the start line.

So: **18–20 km with 6–8 km continuous at goal effort, run by feel, HR ≤ ~166.** Scaled down from 8–10 km at a prescribed pace. Its jobs are to rehearse fuelling and rhythm, to give the legs one race-effort touch, and to sanity-check that nothing hurts. It sets no goal and cancels nothing.

## The goal

The Sep 7 file demoted the target to "beat 2:24:07, sub-2:20 as upside" — written on fever-free day 1, with no data, in the same pessimistic pass that cut the volume. That demotion was premature, and this file does not reinstate a promotion in its place. **Neither number is decided in advance.**

Race it the way this plan has always said to race it: **HR-capped effort, pace as the output.** Out at HR 165–168, honest through the hills, and race the last 10 km. The on-course reference is Patrik's own 2025 race — 4:44/km at HR 168 for 2:24:07. If three weeks of rebuilt volume have landed, the pace at that heart rate will show it in the first 10 km and sub-2:20 takes care of itself. If they haven't, the same effort still beats 2:24 on the back half. There is no version of this where a number set on Sep 18 helps.
