- **Aug 7 (14.0 km, ASICS Superblast 3):** `recovery` — 81% easy, negative split, drift 0.44 bpm/km. Grade-adjusted speed-per-heartbeat against four matched same-terrain runs from June/July ranks **2nd of 5, statistically indistinguishable from the best**. Overall GAP 5:27/km at HR 134 vs the near-identical Jun 18 route at 5:26/km and HR 136 — **same pace, 2 bpm cheaper, pre-niggle vs now.** Uphill efficiency was the best of the entire comparison set.

**The one measurable deficit is descent-specific and small.** Within-run downhill:flat efficiency ratio 0.913 — lowest of the five runs, but Jun 18 was 0.9145 and Jul 31 0.9171, so it is the bottom of the range rather than an outlier. Raw descent pace ran ~4 s/km worse than flat relative to baseline, widening to **8–14 s/km on the steepest pitches**. He is not bailing out: the run's steepest pitch (−7.7%) was covered at 4:48/km, the fastest raw pace of the session.

**Why that deficit matters less than it sounds: Berlin is flat.** Per `races/2026-07-07-mauerweglauf-energiplan.md` the course is asphalt/gravel/broken slabs/sand with a **single named climb (Dörferblick ~km123) already planned as a walk**. **Caveat, stated plainly:** n = 5 matched runs, small margins, all at 10–14 km. This rules out a *large* efficiency loss at short distance. It says nothing about hour 12 of a 161 km race — which is the question that actually matters and which no available data can answer.

## The niggle, reframed — this is the section to re-read

The Jul 31 sync called it a "left hip complex" and flagged the multi-site same-side pattern as coherent but unexplained. **The disc theory explains it.**

- Anterior (hip flexor) + lateral (TFL/glute-med/upper ITB) + under-knee, **all left, all at once**, is a **lumbar referral distribution** — not three overuse injuries that coincidentally co-occurred on one side. Three simultaneous independent tendinopathies was always the weaker hypothesis.
- **Implication 1 (reassuring):** referred symptoms do not reliably behave like mechanical overuse — they don't necessarily worsen monotonically with mileage until they stop you. They fluctuate with position, load and fatigue. Consistent with a marked improvement over five days of rest.
- **Implication 2 (the real risk, and it is specific):** the danger is **not proportional to distance — it is tied to when posture collapses.** A back that is already compensating at km 14 fresh is a different back at hour 14 with a dropped trunk and a tilted pelvis. Patrik's own answer is the honest one: *"It doesn't change my stride during a shortish run (10–14 km). Not sure how it affects the gait when I'm tired."*
- **Implication 3:** this makes posture, early walk breaks and core engagement **strategic**, not cosmetic. It also rhymes with the standing note on the Megablast in `config/training.json` — that its assist is form-dependent and fades when the runner relaxes. Same underlying theme: this athlete's chain degrades when the trunk gives up.

**Status as of today:** improving but present. Noticeable on jogging, **especially downhill**. Does not alter stride at 10–14 km. Behaviour under multi-hour fatigue: **unknown**.

**Treat the disc theory as a working hypothesis, not a diagnosis** — it is a hands-on practitioner's read, not imaging. It is the best explanation available and it should shape the race plan, but it should not be spoken of as established fact.

## Shoes — the decision and the reasoning

**Starting shoe: ASICS Superblast 3.** Backup: **Adidas Evo SL Silver**, drop bag **VP6 Ruderclub Oberhavel, km 33**.

Why this is defensible despite being a late change to a shoe with ~24 km on it:

1. **This is not "proven shoe vs unproven shoe."** It is *a shoe with a known failure mode* versus *a shoe with unknown failure modes*. The Evo SL's instability-driven foot fatigue is a real, experienced, late-race problem. "Proven" overstates it.
2. **"Breaking in" is not the actual task.** Supercritical foams need no mechanical break-in. What needs testing is fit, hotspots and whether the platform suits him over duration — answered by **one longish run, not by accumulated mileage.** Piling on Superblast km has no upside and costs hip recovery.
3. **Stability is protective here, not merely comfortable.** If the back is compensating and the left chain is doing extra work, a wider, more stable platform reduces the demand on exactly the structures that are complaining. Patrik's instinct is better-founded than "these feel nicer."
4. **Sizing is right.** One size up from the Megablast — consistent with the size-up logic already applied to the Evo SL Silver for hour-15 foot swell.

**Why km 33 and not km 70 for the backup:** the failure the backup guards against is **early** — hotspot, blister, arch, "this is wrong" — and that is knowable by km 20–35. The failure that shows at km 70+ is general foot fatigue, which is *the Evo SL's own weakness*; swapping into it late means swapping into the shoe whose known problem is late-race feet. At km 33 a wrong call still leaves 128 km in a shoe he has covered 50 km in. At km 70 he has committed half the race to a possibly-wrong shoe.

**Optional third pair — Altra Torin 8 at VP17, km 102.** Wide, stable, cushioned, thoroughly known. A dry fresh pair on swollen feet with 60 km to go, at a stop already being made to collect mandatory night gear (headlamp + reflective vest). Close to free; Patrik's call.

**Gate from Sun Aug 9:** any hotspot, blister, arch fatigue or foot fatigue in the Superblast by ~90 minutes → **it does not start the race**, and the Evo SL reverts to starting shoe. Clean run → it starts.

## Race-day hip protocol

New material, driven by the disc/compensation theory. This belongs alongside the energiplan, not instead of it.

- **Posture is the primary defence.** The failure mode is trunk collapse, so run tall with hips forward and make it an explicit check-in at every VP — same cadence as the fuelling checks. When posture goes, the compensation gets worse; that is the mechanism.
- **Deliberate early walk breaks, before they are forced.** Walking unloads differently and resets pelvic position. Standard ultra advice, with extra force here: this is not just energy management, it is posture management. Walk Dörferblick (~km123) as already planned, but do not wait for it to be the first walk.
- **Go out easy — with more conviction than usual.** Time banked early is worth less than a back that is still holding at km 100. Jul 30 already demonstrated what happens when the early kilometres run hot on a compromised day.
- **Reassess formally at the two big drop bags — km 70 (halfway) and km 102.** These are natural decision points and they are already stops.
- **Neuro red flags = stop, not push through.** Numbness or pins-and-needles down the leg; any loss of strength (knee buckling, foot slapping or dragging); symptoms going bilateral; any bowel or bladder change. These are categorically different from muscular pain and from the current awareness-level symptoms. Ordinary aching and the familiar hip/knee grumble are not on this list. **Confirm this list with the naprapat on Aug 10–11** rather than relying on it as written here.
- **DNF over injury, every time.** The standing rule from the season plan. Berlin is the A-race, but a disc is a multi-season problem and Lidingöloppet (Sep 26) plus everything after it sits on the other side of the scale.
