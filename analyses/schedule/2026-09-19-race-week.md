### Why the weekend goes up, not down

The instinct at seven days out is to start tapering. Don't — not this weekend.

The limiter into this race was never intensity. It was four weeks of lost aerobic volume, and the last nine days added three hard sessions while dropping ~24 km of easy running (`../syncs/2026-09-19-week-sync-sep12-18.md`). Sharpening is banked. Volume is not.

Sunday's 16–18 km is safe specifically *because* of what follows it: **three complete days of rest immediately after.** There is no scenario where a Z2 run six days out, followed by 72 hours of nothing, is not fully absorbed by Saturday. The forced break is what makes the fuller weekend affordable — so use it.

With this weekend, the Sep 14–20 week lands near **~62 km** against its ~70 km target, instead of the 33 km it currently sits at.

### Why Thursday is mandatory, and what it is not

After 72 hours of zero, legs go flat and the aerobic system goes quiet. Thursday is not there to add fitness — nothing done this week can add fitness — it is there to **wake the system up and re-find race rhythm** before the athlete has to produce it under a bib.

Three by three minutes is nine minutes of work. That is deliberately trivial. Its jobs:

1. Remind the legs and the cardiovascular system what HR 165–168 feels like, so the opening kilometres on Saturday are calibrated by feel and not by guesswork.
2. **Put the race shoe on race effort.** This build has never run a goal-effort segment in a Lidingö candidate — Sep 18's 4:40 GAP block was in the Megablast, which is not a race option (narrow toe-box). Thursday is the last chance to find out the Tecton X3 is fine at race intensity while there is still time to change the plan.
3. Confirm nothing hurts.

**What Thursday must not become:** a session. Not a tempo, not a time trial, not a "let's see where I am". See below.

## The one instruction that matters this week

**The three lost days are not a debt. Do not repay them.**

This build has a documented, repeated pattern — diagnosed as a planning error on Sep 10, then reproduced from the execution side Sep 15–18 — of **cutting easy volume and adding hard sessions** when a week comes under pressure. Tuesday's "no VO2 regardless of the group format" became 4 × 4 min to 182 bpm. Thursday's "easy 12–14 km + strides" became a race effort finishing at max HR 184.

The mirror of that failure, in this week's shape, is obvious and it is the single most likely way this race gets worse between now and Saturday: arriving at Thursday after three restless days off, feeling undertrained, and turning a 9-minute rhythm touch into a workout that proves something.

Nothing done on Sep 24 or Sep 25 can improve Sep 26. It can only subtract freshness. If Thursday feels too easy, **that is the session working correctly.**

The same applies in the other direction: if the legs feel flat and heavy on Thursday, that is 72 hours of rest and not a fitness verdict. Do not react to it, and do not add anything on Friday to compensate.

## Race day — how to run it

The goal is unchanged from Sep 10 and is deliberately still not a number. **HR-capped effort, pace as the output.** Out at **HR 165–168**, honest through the hills, race the last 10 km. The on-course reference is Patrik's own 2025 race: 4:44/km at HR 168 for 2:24:07.

Sep 18 added one concrete execution rule that was not available before.

**Concede pace on the climbs. Do not defend GAP uphill.**

The data (`../syncs/2026-09-19-week-sync-sep12-18.md`): at aerobic effort a +6% grade costs **+2.7 bpm** while pace falls 15% — a well-regulated response, the hill paid in pace. Near threshold that inverts. On the one real climb inside the Sep 18 surge, pace dropped to 5:06 while **GAP was held at 4:31 — he pushed** — and HR went from 160 to **171**, the session's ceiling.

So at race intensity there is almost no heart-rate headroom on a climb: the hill gets charged twice, to pace *and* to heart rate. Lidingöloppet carries far more vert than that run's 161 m over 16.7 km. **The 4:40 GAP demonstrated on rolling road only coexists with a 168 cap if the uphills are given away on purpose.** Let the climbs be slow, take the heart rate back on the descents, and spend what's left after 20 km.

Patrik's own note — *"pulsen går upp direkt när det börjar luta det minsta uppåt"* — is false at easy effort and true at race effort. It is a real constraint, and the answer to it is pacing discipline in the first half, not fitness.
