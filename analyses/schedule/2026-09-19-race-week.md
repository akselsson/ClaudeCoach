# Race week — Lidingöloppet, Sat Sep 26

### Why the weekend goes up, not down

The instinct at seven days out is to start tapering. Don't — not this weekend.

The limiter into this race was never intensity. It was four weeks of lost aerobic volume, and the last nine days added three hard sessions while dropping ~24 km of easy running (`../syncs/2026-09-19-week-sync-sep12-18.md`). Sharpening is banked. Volume is not.

The long run is safe at six or seven days out specifically *because* of what follows it: **three days without running.** There is no scenario where a Z2 run, followed by 72 hours of no mechanical load, is not absorbed by Saturday. The forced break is what makes the fuller weekend affordable — so use it.

One caveat added in the amendment below: those 72 hours are **not clean rest**, because of the alcohol and sleep debt riding on them. That does not change the mechanical argument — the legs still get their break — but it does mean the systemic recovery this week is worse than "three days off" sounds.

With this weekend, the Sep 14–20 week lands near **~62 km** against its ~70 km target, instead of the 33 km it currently sits at.

### The weekend is flipped (amendment, same day)

There is a 50th birthday party tonight, with alcohol and a short night.

The original version of this file put the 16–18 km on Sunday. That would have run the single most important remaining session of the build on a dehydrated, sleep-deprived body — and it was the one day of the week where the scheduling was still free. So swap them: **the long run happens today, Saturday, before the party. Sunday becomes a short forgiving jog.**

Three reasons this is strictly better and not just rearrangement:

1. **The big run gets the good day.** Today is the last day this week that is not compromised by either alcohol or a forced constraint. Spend it on the session that matters.
2. **It matches the standing schedule anyway.** Saturday is the B2B continuation day in `../preferences/2026-05-03-training-preferences.md`, stacked behind the Friday long run. Fri 16.7 km + Sat 16–18 km is an ordinary back-to-back by this build's standards, and it is exactly the aerobic block the week is short of.
3. **The hungover run becomes low-stakes.** 8–10 km by feel costs nothing if it goes badly. 17 km by feel, badly, on a Sunday, would have been the week's last aerobic session executed at its worst.

**If today is already gone** — no time before the party — then keep the longer run on Sunday but cut it to 12–14 km, run it late in the day after rehydrating, and read nothing at all into the heart rate.

**On Sunday, whichever version you run: do not look at HR as a fitness signal.** It will read 5–10 bpm high at any pace. That is alcohol and plasma volume, not detraining, and not a verdict on Saturday. Run by time and feel.

### Why Thursday is mandatory, and what it is not

After 72 hours of zero, legs go flat and the aerobic system goes quiet. Thursday is not there to add fitness — nothing done this week can add fitness — it is there to **wake the system up and re-find race rhythm** before the athlete has to produce it under a bib.

Three by three minutes is nine minutes of work. That is deliberately trivial. Its jobs:

1. Recalibrate what race effort feels like, so Saturday's opening kilometres are paced by feel and not by guesswork. **Run the first of the three reps at HR ≤163 rather than 166–168** — the km 0–13 cap is the part of the race plan that is hardest to feel and easiest to overshoot, so rehearse that, not just the hard end.
2. **Put the race shoe on race effort.** This build has never run a goal-effort segment in a Lidingö candidate — Sep 18's 4:40 GAP block was in the Megablast, which is not a race option (narrow toe-box). Thursday is the last chance to find out the Tecton X3 is fine at race intensity while there is still time to change the plan.
3. Confirm nothing hurts.

**What Thursday must not become:** a session. Not a tempo, not a time trial, not a "let's see where I am". See below.

## The one instruction that matters this week

**The three lost days are not a debt. Do not repay them.**

This build has a documented, repeated pattern — diagnosed as a planning error on Sep 10, then reproduced from the execution side Sep 15–18 — of **cutting easy volume and adding hard sessions** when a week comes under pressure. Tuesday's "no VO2 regardless of the group format" became 4 × 4 min to 182 bpm. Thursday's "easy 12–14 km + strides" became a race effort finishing at max HR 184.

The mirror of that failure, in this week's shape, is obvious and it is the single most likely way this race gets worse between now and Saturday: arriving at Thursday after three restless days off, feeling undertrained, and turning a 9-minute rhythm touch into a workout that proves something.

Nothing done on Sep 24 or Sep 25 can improve Sep 26. It can only subtract freshness. If Thursday feels too easy, **that is the session working correctly.**

The same applies in the other direction: if the legs feel flat and heavy on Thursday, that is 72 hours of rest and not a fitness verdict. Do not react to it, and do not add anything on Friday to compensate.

## Race day — how to run it

The goal is unchanged from Sep 10 and is deliberately still not a number. **HR-capped effort, pace as the output.**

**The full course model, terrain analysis and hike list live in `../races/2026-09-19-lidingoloppet-course-model.md`.** That file is the authority for race day; this is the summary.

### The short version

Patrik's recollection was *"sub 160 — I think that's what I did last year."* The activity says **avg HR 168.3** for 2:24:07; sub-160 appears in km 1 only. But his course knowledge then corrected the reading in the other direction, and a grade-adjusted reanalysis settled it:

- 2025's 43 s/km pace decay is **~16 s/km terrain, ~7 s/km walking, ~20 s/km genuine fade.** Terrain and strategy account for just over half.
- Decoupling is ~10%, of which 4–5% is expected cardiac drift over 2:24 at 91% of max HR. **The real fade is ~5%** — meaningful, but far smaller than the pace column implies.
- **The fade is at km 20–25, not at the finish.** Two of the course's five biggest climbs arrive back-to-back there and were run rather than hiked. From km 24 on, where he did hike, efficiency stopped deteriorating.
- **He already walked in 2025** — 275 s, 240 s of it in the last 10 km. Walking is not a new strategy.

### The cap — block averages, not instantaneous ceilings

**Lidingöloppet's markers count kilometres REMAINING** (marker = 30 − distance run), so the plan is given in markers — that is what the boards on the day will say.

| **marker** | km run | HR | terrain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **30 → 17** | 0–13 | **≤ 163 avg** | Moderate rolling, then the flattest part. Killingebacken at marker 25.7. |
| **17 → 10** | 13–20 | **≤ 166 avg** | The roller-coaster — spikes over 170 are expected, ignore them |
| **10 → 5** | 20–25 | **≤ 168** | The decisive section |
| **5 → 0** | 25–30 | **≤ 170**, free from the top of Abborrbacken | |

**Why 163 rather than 160 or 168.** In 2025 the first 5 km at HR 161.7 produced the **fastest grade-adjusted running of the whole race (4:21 GAP)**. The next 5 km at HR 168.0 produced **slower** running (4:26). Six extra beats bought nothing. 161.7 is empirically where he runs this course best, so the cap sits just above it — and a flat sub-160 would have over-corrected for a fade that turned out to be ~5%.

### Hike these four climbs

1. **marker 9.5 → 9.0** (km 20.5, +30.6 m at 5.7%) — **NEW; ran it in 2025, and this is where the fade began**
2. **marker 8.0 → 7.4** (km 22.0, +26.0 m at 4.2%) — **NEW; same reason**
3. **marker 5.0 → 4.2 — Abborrbacken** (km 25.0–25.8, +48.4 m at 6.6% from sea level) — hiked in 2025, keep doing it
4. **marker 1.6 → 0.5** (km 28.4–29.5, +32.2 m) — hiked in 2025, keep doing it

**The Grönsta gärde marker-10 board is the cue: from there, the next two hills get walked.**

The prescription is not "walk more at the end" — he already does that and it works. It is **arrive at km 20 with more left, and hike the two climbs at km 20.5 and km 22 as well.**

**Caveat carried from the alcohol/sleep section:** heart rate will read high all day this year, so these caps will deliver slower pace than they look like they should. Run them anyway.

### Why the climbs get conceded

**Concede pace on the climbs. Do not defend GAP uphill.**

The data (`../syncs/2026-09-19-week-sync-sep12-18.md`): at aerobic effort a +6% grade costs **+2.7 bpm** while pace falls 15% — a well-regulated response, the hill paid in pace. Near threshold that inverts. On the one real climb inside the Sep 18 surge, pace dropped to 5:06 while **GAP was held at 4:31 — he pushed** — and HR went from 160 to **171**, the session's ceiling.

So at race intensity there is almost no heart-rate headroom on a climb: the hill gets charged twice, to pace *and* to heart rate. Lidingöloppet carries far more vert than that run's 161 m over 16.7 km. **The 4:40 GAP demonstrated on rolling road only coexists with a 168 cap if the uphills are given away on purpose.** Let the climbs be slow, take the heart rate back on the descents, and spend what's left after 20 km.

Patrik's own note — *"pulsen går upp direkt när det börjar luta det minsta uppåt"* — is false at easy effort and true at race effort. It is a real constraint, and the answer to it is pacing discipline in the first half, not fitness.

### What the HR cap will feel like this year

Worth saying in advance so it isn't a surprise at km 5. Carrying sleep debt and four nights of alcohol, **heart rate at any given pace will sit high**. Running honestly to the staged cap will therefore produce a slower pace than Sep 18's 4:40 GAP implied — possibly noticeably slower, and the ≤163 opening block especially so.

**Run the cap anyway.** The elevated heart rate is not a measurement error; it reflects genuine physiological stress, and the cap is what stops that stress turning into a blow-up at km 22. The temptation will be to decide the HR is "lying" and chase the pace instead. That is the failure mode, and it is the same instinct — defending a number at the body's expense — that produced the Sep 15 and Sep 17 sessions.

And to be plain about the goal, since the file otherwise refuses to name one: **sub-2:20 is unlikely off a 33 km week, three unplanned hard sessions, three zero days and four compromised nights.** Chasing it is how this race goes badly. Beating 2:24:07 on an HR-capped effort is well within reach, and the season plan's framing — a B-race, *"a fast 30k off ultra fitness"* — is the honest one. Race the effort and read the clock afterwards.

That is potentially **four compromised nights in the five days before the race**, and it lands on exactly the physiology this whole block has been trying to rebuild.

**Why it bites harder than usual here.** The entire premise of `2026-09-10-volume-rebuild.md` is plasma-volume expansion — that is the adaptation the extra easy kilometres were bought to produce, and it is why resting HR and HR-at-pace were elevated in the first place. **Alcohol is a diuretic and works directly against it.** Four nights of drinking in race week will measurably shrink the thing the last two weeks of running were for. This is not a general health caution; it is specific to the one adaptation this build is chasing.

Sleep debt adds the second half: it raises heart rate at a given workload and raises perceived effort at a given pace. On a race run to an HR cap, that shows up as **slower pace for the same heart rate** — the cap will simply deliver less.

### What to actually do

Not abstinence. This is a B-race (`../season-plan/2026-05-03-season-plan-overview.md`: *"race Lidingöloppet on whatever fitness remains — don't try to PR"*), and a friend's 50th and a daughter's 18th are not negotiable against a sharpener. The cost is real, bounded, and worth paying. But it should be paid deliberately rather than by accident:

- **Tonight (7 days out):** cheapest night of the week. Enjoy it. Water between drinks, eat properly, and protect sleep *duration* even if quality is poor — going to bed at 1am beats 4am by more than the drinks cost.
- **Mon–Tue offsite (5 and 4 days out):** **this is the one to moderate.** Not zero, but a deliberate ceiling and an early exit on at least one of the two nights. These are the nights with the least social cost to leaving early and the highest physiological price for staying.
- **Wed, her 18th (3 days out):** the last night that can absorb a late one. Don't manage it. Be there properly.
- **Thu and Fri nights: clean.** No alcohol, sleep is the priority.

**Protect Thursday night hardest.** The night that best predicts race-day performance is the night *two* before, not the night before — pre-race nerves tend to wreck the last night regardless, and the body is running on what it banked the night before that. Thursday Sep 24 is that night.

**Rehydrate with electrolytes, not just water**, from Wednesday onward. Plasma volume is the specific target; plain water alone is a weaker tool for it than water plus sodium.
