# Post-illness re-prescription — Sep 7 → Lidingöloppet Sep 26

## What changed

The Aug 28 block assumed Aug 29 – Sep 6 would be a clean easy-rebuild week ending in a Fri Sep 4 gate check. Instead: near-total rest (one strength session), one hard trail day taken while incubating, and a fever Fri evening → Sun morning. See `../syncs/2026-09-07-illness-week-sync.md`.

Today (Mon Sep 7) is **fever-free day 1**. Lidingöloppet is **19 days out**.

Two consequences drive everything below:

1. **The recovery gate never got checked.** Fri Sep 4 was supposed to answer "is the easy-pace HR drift gone?" It answered a different question entirely. We still don't know where the post-ultra autonomic recovery actually sits — now with an illness layered on top.
2. **The runway is halved.** The block budgeted two quality weeks plus race week. One of those weeks is gone. There is now exactly **one** quality week (Sep 14–20) and **one** race-pace session before the start line.

The engine evidence is untouched — Aug 27's no-fade threshold reps and Aug 28's near-parity shape test still stand. What's lost is sharpness and, more decisively, the *time to convert* the engine into 30 km race pace.

## The goal default flips

The Aug 28 file framed this as "A-goal sub-2:20, decided by the Sep 18 workout." That framing assumed a full build. Revised:

- **Working target: beat 2:24:07** (LL 2025, 4:44/km, avg HR 168). This is now the plan of record.
- **Upside: sub-2:20** (~4:36/km) — still live, but it has to be *earned* by the Sep 18 workout, not merely not-disproved by it.
- **Floor: race by effort, no time goal.** New branch. If the return week doesn't come clean, LL is a hard training day on a great course, and that's a fine outcome in a season whose A-race is already banked.

This is a demotion of ambition, not of the athlete. Sub-2:20 asked for ~8 s/km on a 2025 race already run at HR 168; that needed everything to go right, and ten days just went wrong.

### Gate 1 — moved to Thu Sep 10

It was Fri Sep 11, but Friday is now a trail run and trail pace can't be read against a pace-based HR gate. So the gate goes to Thursday, on flat road, where the number means something. It is also now the **go/no-go for Friday**.

Pass = **all** of:
- ~5:10–5:20/km sits at **≤ ~145 bpm without ramping** across the run (the original gate, unchanged);
- resting HR back to baseline (~44), not sitting 8–10 bpm high;
- cough gone, no chest tightness;
- right hip flexor silent.

**Pass →** Ursvik Friday as prescribed, then Stage 2 as written.
**Fail →** Friday becomes an easy 45 min on the flat, the Ursvik run moves to Sun Sep 13 at the earliest (or is dropped), the quality week is cancelled, and LL is run at the **floor**: even effort at HR ~160–165, no time goal. Do not attempt a decision workout on a body that failed the gate.

### Why Ursvik on Friday — and why it survives the illness rules

This is a change of mind, and the reasoning is worth recording. The Aug 28 block and the first cut of this file both prescribed LL preparation entirely as **road-pace work** — Kundo reps, a marathon-effort block, a pace-measurable decision workout. Lidingöloppet is a hilly gravel/trail race. Nothing in the plan loaded the legs the way the race will: sustained climbing, and especially the **eccentric cost of descending**, which is the adaptation that takes the longest to acquire and the one that wrecks quads late in a hilly 30 km. That was a hole in the plan.

There is also no other slot for it. Fri Sep 18 must stay pace-measurable (you cannot read 4:36/km off Ursvik), and Sep 19–20 sits in the shadow of the decision workout and race week. **Fri Sep 11 is the only remaining day that can carry terrain work with enough runway to adapt — 15 days.**

Patrik's read that the legs are recovered from the 100-miler is supported: no on-rep fade at D+12 (Aug 27), near-parity with the 2025 shape test at D+13 (Aug 28). The limiter after Mauerweglauf was never musculoskeletal. And critically, **the illness risk and the vert are not the same axis.** Post-viral risk tracks systemic cardiovascular load — HR and duration — not elevation gain. Climbing at a capped HR is cheap on the axis that matters and expensive only on the axis that has recovered. That's why this run is allowed and the Tue Sep 8 Kundo is not.

### Rules for the Friday run

Non-negotiable, in priority order:

1. **HR ≤ 150 for the entire run.** Hike the steep sections if that's what the cap costs. On this terrain the cap will feel absurdly easy on the flats and genuinely restrictive on the climbs — that is the session working correctly.
2. **2:15 hard stop.** Duration is the governor, not the loop. If the HR cap makes the pace slower than expected and the full ~23 km won't fit inside 2:15, **cut the loop short — do not lift the cap to make the distance.** Getting 19 km at HR 145 beats 23 km at 160.
3. **Zero segment chasing.** This is the specific failure mode from Sep 4 — *"fick ändå feeling och körde på vid segmenten"* — and it happened while incubating a fever. Same course, same temptation, five days off a fever. No PRs, no efforts, don't look at the watch for anything but HR.
4. **Halfway checkpoint.** At the point where you turned for home on Sep 4: if HR is drifting up at constant effort, or anything feels off, take the short way back. A truncated Ursvik run still delivers most of the descending load.
5. **Descend under control but don't baby it.** The downhills are the point — they're the stimulus road running can't give. Relaxed and rolling, not braked and not hammered.

Friday also functions as a **second reading** on recovery: if HR drifts abnormally on the flat opening kilometres, that contradicts Thursday's gate and the quality week gets reconsidered.

### Gate 2 — the decision workout (Fri Sep 18)

Scaled down from the original 12 km continuous to **8–10 km**. Eight days out, on a first hard long run after a fever, 12 km at race pace is a session you recover *from* rather than one you learn *from*. Ten kilometres answers the same question at a fraction of the cost.

- Block holds **4:36–4:40/km at HR ≤ ~166** and reads as repeatable for 30 km → **race the upside**: even effort at sub-2:20, HR ~168 ceiling through the hills.
- Block costs **HR 170+**, or pace fades inside the block → **race the working target**: out at 2025 effort (HR 165–168), race the last 10 km, beat 2:24 on the back half.
- Block can't be completed, or HR is still offset from pace → **the floor**: HR ~160–165 the whole way, no clock.

Pacing philosophy is unchanged and non-negotiable: **LL is raced by HR-capped effort, with pace as the output.** The terrain makes km splits noise; the on-course reference is last year's own race (4:44/km at HR 168).

## Stop rules

Back off immediately — and treat the gate as failed — on any of: return of fever, cough worsening or moving into the chest, chest tightness or pain, breathlessness out of proportion to the effort, palpitations, or resting HR persistently 8–10 bpm above baseline. A febrile illness is the one context where "push through it" has a genuinely bad tail. There is no version of Lidingöloppet worth that.

Carried-over watch items:
- **Right hip flexor.** If it reacts to any quality session, drop the next quality slot entirely rather than trading it for volume.
- **Max HR stays 184 in config.** The 184 recorded Sep 4 was reached at 6:03/km while incubating a fever — an illness artefact, not a new ceiling. Don't recalibrate off it.
- **Race shoe still open.** Gravel/trail/road mix. Decide in race week; 2025's choice is in the Strava record.
