# Activity sync — Sun Aug 9: the last long run

Gear: ASICS Superblast 3
Distance 17.0 km · 98 min · 5:46/km · avg HR 134.8 / max 149 · 189 m gain / 187 m loss · cadence 173 spm · Strava id 19666148061

Executing `schedule/2026-08-07-weekly-review.md`, which prescribed 16–18 km (~90 min) easy Z1 on a flat route in the Superblast 3, carrying three separate jobs. **All three are now answered, and all three came back green.** Characterized via `characterize-activity` in a sub-agent.

**Read in one line: the last diagnostic run of the build produced no bad news, which at six days out is exactly the result worth having.**

## The three jobs

**1. The Superblast 3 fit gate — PASSED, clean.** No hotspots, no blisters, no arch fatigue, no toe-box pressure at ~90 minutes. The Aug 7 review set this as a hard gate rather than a preference: any of those findings and the shoe does not start. Nothing to report. **The ASICS Superblast 3 is confirmed as the starting shoe for Berlin**, with the Evo SL Silver as the early-abort backup at the km 33 drop bag. The shoe now has ~41 km on it, all of it in the last four days, and that is enough — supercritical foams need no mechanical break-in, and what needed testing (fit, hotspots, platform over duration) is what was tested.

**2. The hydration rehearsal — DONE, as written.** 1× 90 g U Sport portion split across **both** 450 ml softflasks (~45 g each, ~10% mix), drinking every 10 min. This closes an item open since **Jul 30**, where the flask was mixed at roughly double concentration in two-thirds the fluid and the day ended in GI shutdown. The energiplan's actual serving rule had never been run until today. It has now, and it sat fine.

**3. The hip under duration — held.** Patrik's own words: *"Fortfarande känningar i vänster höft/knä men aldrig smärta. Det är mer att det är något som inte känns helt normalt."* Same character as Aug 7 — awareness, not pain — now sustained across 98 minutes rather than 76. This is the longest look available at "how does it behave tired," and it is a 90-minute proxy for a 14-hour question, so it should not be over-read. But the direction is right.

## What the numbers say

- **Effort:** `recovery` by intensity — 82.4% easy (<140), 17.6% steady (140–150), **nothing above 150.** The Z1 ceiling was broadly honoured. But the tag reads intensity only: at 17 km / 98 min this was the **second-heaviest run of the last 14 days** by load (TRIMP 144, Edwards 117, suffer 50), behind only the Jul 30 50 km.
- **No drift, and a negative split.** Raw drift 0.38 bpm/km against a ~1.5 threshold of concern. Halves: HR 133.4 → 136.1 while pace *improved* 5:50 → 5:39/km. Grade-adjusted efficiency by quarter ran 1.342 → 1.277 → 1.299 → **1.365** — **the last quarter was the strongest of the run.** Nothing faded.
- **The descent penalty is unchanged — this is the finding that matters.** Downhill:flat efficiency ratio **0.949**, against 0.947 on Aug 7 and 0.948 on Aug 6. A flat line across three runs. The one measurable mechanical deficit tracked since the niggle appeared is **not deteriorating**, and uphill:flat (1.011) was the best of the three. On a course whose single named climb is already planned as a walk, this is close to a non-issue.
- **Route was flat-*ish*, not flat.** 189 m gain over 17 km, net zero — but the *roughness* was the lowest of the three recent runs: only 12.4% of distance at |grade| ≥5%, against 22.4% on Aug 7. Grade p5/p95 ±5% vs ±7%. Close enough to the intent.

**The one soft spot, stated plainly:** overall grade-adjusted efficiency was **1.320 m/beat against Aug 7's 1.406** — about 6% down at effectively identical heart rate (134.9 vs 134.4). Taken alone that would be a flag. It is confounded four ways: 98 minutes vs 76, a 10:53 start in August warmth, third run in four days, and deliberate taper restraint. And it is directly contradicted by the run's own shape — a negative split with its best quarter last is not what a compromised chain produces. **Logged, not acted on.** If it were a real efficiency loss it would have shown as fade within the run, and it didn't.

## Decisions

- **Race shoe: ASICS Superblast 3 confirmed.** Gate passed. Evo SL Silver stays as the km 33 backup; the optional Altra Torin 8 at km 102 remains Patrik's call.
- **Hydration protocol: validated as written.** Race it as rehearsed — 90 g across ~900 ml, every 10 minutes. Do not improvise concentration on the day; Jul 30 is what that costs.
- **Race week unchanged.** `schedule/2026-08-07-weekly-review.md` stands as written: off Mon, easy 8 km in the Evo SL Tue, off Wed, 5–6 km shakeout + optional 4×20 s strides Thu, off Fri, race Sat. Nothing in today's run argues for adding or removing anything.
- **The Tue Aug 11 Evo SL leg keeps its purpose but loses its stakes.** It was framed as an A/B against the Superblast. The A/B is now decided, so Tuesday is simply an easy 8 km that happens to be a last sanity-check on the backup shoe's fit. Don't let it become a re-litigation of the shoe choice.
- **Naprapat follow-up is booked for Mon/Tue Aug 10–11.** The three questions from the Aug 7 review go with it, in order of importance: (1) does running 161 km risk actual harm or only discomfort; (2) which symptoms mean stop rather than push through — specifically, confirm or correct the neuro red-flag list in the race-day hip protocol; (3) anything worth doing in the remaining week. **(1) is still the most important unanswered question of this build.**
