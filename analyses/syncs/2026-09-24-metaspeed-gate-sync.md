## The gate: PASSED. Race the Metaspeed.

The Sep 20 amendment made the Metaspeed the default and named one open risk — *"zero runs above 12 m/km ... and it sits alongside his own stability concern on wet descents."* The gate was written to test that, with the rule **clean session → race them, anything sketchy → race the Megablast.**

**Rep 2 is the evidence that matters.** It is the only hard descent the shoe has ever been given: −17.6 m net over 3 minutes, touching **−8.2 % grade**, run at 4:12/km with a 20 s window at 3:42/km — the fastest running in the whole session. That is a genuine test of a carbon plate on a steep descent, and it returned clean on both channels:

- **His report:** *"Everything about the shoes felt great."* No skating, no instability, nothing sketchy, no gravel complaint.
- **The trace:** cadence held **177–180 spm** through the descent, and 173–180 across all three reps with no collapse and no asymmetry. Rep 3, on a uniform 3–5 % climb, split 5:02 / 5:12 / 4:50 / 5:04 / 4:58 / 5:02 — that flatness under load is a clean control signal, not a foot fighting its shoe.

The two uphill reps add the other half: ~26 m of climb each, sustained 3–5 %, with rep 1 finishing on a **+11 %** ramp. The shoe was put on steep ground in both directions and nothing came back bad.

**Decision: the Metaspeed Sky Paris races Saturday.** The Megablast stays as an unused fallback and needs no further testing.

### What the gate did *not* establish

Worth writing down so the record is honest rather than triumphal:

1. **Nine minutes is not two hours twenty.** The reps were steep; the *run* was not hilly — 10.2 m/km by Strava's number, identical to Sunday's flat park run and a little over half the course's ~18 m/km. (Stream-derived density runs higher at 15.0 m/km, but the rep-level figures in this file are stream-derived and must be scaled by ~0.68 before comparison with the 18 m/km course number.) For contrast, Sep 17's terrain session was 18.6 m/km — *that* was the Lidingö-density run, and it was in the Tecton. So the "zero runs above 12 m/km" gap is narrowed by a concentrated sample, not closed.
2. **The shoe's known failure mode is late, and untestable this week.** His own hypothesis is that the Metaspeed stops helping once form breaks down — the +45.2 s/km residual on the 93 km. Nothing done on Sep 24 can probe that. The reassurance remains structural rather than empirical: 30 km / ~2h20 sits far below the threshold, and even the 42.8 km came in at −22.6.

Neither of these changes the decision. They mean the residual risk on Saturday is **late-race, not descent-related** — which is the opposite of the risk the gate was written to check, and is already covered by the staged HR caps and the hike-the-climbs list.

## The session itself: the pattern did not repeat

This is the more consequential finding for Saturday, and it deserves to be stated plainly.

`2026-09-19-race-week.md` called out one failure mode above all others: *"arriving at Thursday after three restless days off, feeling undertrained, and turning a 9-minute rhythm touch into a workout that proves something."* The file documented that exact pattern twice in this build — Tuesday's VO2 ban becoming 4 × 4 min to 182, Thursday's easy 12–14 km becoming a race effort to max HR.

Two supporting signals that the body is in good shape rather than merely being spared:

- **Heart-rate recovery was excellent** — 60 s HRR of 27 / 34 / 31 bpm after the three reps, dropping to 125 / 111 / 119. That is not an athlete who is cooked after 72 hours off.
- **No flat-and-heavy reading.** The file explicitly predicted Thursday might feel terrible and pre-emptively told him to ignore it. It didn't, and he reported no such thing. That is the second time in five days a pre-discounted negative prediction failed to materialise (the Sunday hangover-HR penalty was the first). Worth carrying into race morning: **read the day, don't pre-discount it.**

### The HR bands were inverted, and it doesn't matter

Prescribed easiest-first (rep 1 ≤163, reps 2–3 at 166–168). Delivered hardest-first:

| | Rep 1 | Rep 2 | Rep 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terrain (his report, confirmed by the stream) | all uphill | downhill → flat | all uphill |
| Elevation | **+21.2 m**, closing at +11 % | **−17.6 m**, min −8.2 % | **+23.2 m**, uniform 3–5 % |
| Avg pace / GAP | 4:35 / 3:44 | 4:12 / 4:38 | 5:02 / 4:00 |
| Avg HR / peak | 155.7 / **168** | 148.2 / 156 | 158.3 / 163 |
| Last 30 s avg HR | 166.0 | 153.0 | 159.8 |
| vs. prescribed band | **+5 over a ≤163 cap** | **−10 to −12** | **−3 to −5** |

- **Rep 1 overshot** because it finished on a +11 % ramp with HR still climbing. Terrain feature, not a pacing blowup.
- **Rep 2 undershot by a lot** and is fully explained: *a 3 min descent cannot drive HR to 167.* Mechanically it was a downhill speed rep, not a race-effort rep — which is exactly why it was the useful one for the shoe question.
- **Rep 3 undershot by ~4 bpm** and this one is partly real: HR plateaued flat at 161–162 from +60 s, so it wasn't still lagging upward. A modest genuine shortfall.

**None of this is a fitness verdict and none of it needs correcting.** The file's own words: *"the reps are for calibration, not stimulus."* Calibration was achieved — he has now felt 166–168 on a climb in the race shoe, two days out. Rep 1's GAP of 3:44/km should not be read as fitness evidence; Minetti-based grade adjustment over-rewards steep climbs. It means "this hurt like sub-4:00 pace", which is the right sensation to have re-found.
