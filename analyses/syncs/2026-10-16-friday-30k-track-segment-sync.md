## Same venue, last year: the two 40 km+ track runs of Oct 2025

Patrik asked how today compares with the two long runs he did at Sollentunavallen in the same slot of last year's PRT build. Both were run entirely on the track (every GPS sample within ~90 m of the centre): **Oct 17 2025**, 40.0 km in 3:37, HR 133/163, Evo SL (normal-fit pair), with a training partner for 3 h then a fast finish out of boredom; **Oct 24 2025**, 43.5 km in 4:00, HR 135/148, Boston 13, "regnigt, blåsigt och småkallt", six stops. HR streams clean on both, no wrist-dropout signatures. Activities 16168000802 and 16239626649.

A ramp flatters pace-at-HR by a few s/km because HR lags, so 4:56–4:59 in a progression and 5:02 held steady are the same number. Today's came an hour earlier in the run, which cuts the other way. Call it level.

**At easy HR: ~10 s/km slower today, surface explains some of it.** Pace at HR 133–137: 5:26 and 5:23 on the track last year; 5:41 on today's rolling road, 5:35 on the flat samples only. Patrik runs faster on the track than on tarmac by his own account, so the honest residual is small and could be zero. Not faster than last year at easy HR either.

**Durability: last year is the benchmark, this year is untested.** Oct 17 2025 ran 32 km at 5:28 with HR flat at 130–133 before the finish, no drift at all. Oct 24 drifted from ~km 22–27 in the rain (+7.6 bpm Q1→Q4 at equal pace) and still held 5:29. Today's 22 km preamble was drift-free but is too short to say anything about km 25–45. That is what the Oct 30 36 km and the peak-block 40–45 km runs have to show: flat HR at ~5:25–5:30 on the track through 35 km.

**Fuelling was not like for like (Patrik, 2026-10-16).** Last year's track longs were fuelling rehearsals: 45 g carbs every 30 min, i.e. 90 g/h, from start to finish. Today was ~100 g in total over 2:56, roughly 35 g/h. That tilts the comparison in today's favour on both rows: easy pace at HR 133–137 over 2 h on a third of the carbohydrate, and a race-effort segment after 22 km on near-empty rather than after 34 km fully fed. It also means today's drift-free 22 km says less about durability than it would have fed, and more about aerobic economy. From Oct 23 the block runs 60 g/h and from Oct 30 90 g/h, which restores like-for-like with last year from the 32 km run on.

**Where the long-run progression sits.** Last year by Oct 17 the long was 40 km; this year it is 30 km in week 2, 36 km on Oct 30, 40–45 km in the peak block. Three to four weeks behind on long-run distance, by design after Mauerweglauf and Lidingö, at equal pace-at-effort. The fitness is there; the specific endurance has to be rebuilt in weeks 4–6.

**One thing for the Nov 20 race plan, not for now.** Last year's training-track pace at HR ~147 was ~4:56–5:02 and the race's fastest stretch was 5:33–5:41 at HR 149–152 on the PRT loop (km 31–60). That is a 35–40 s/km gap between the track number and race pace at matched HR. **Patrik's note (2026-10-16): he felt a bit off during PRT 2025 and the HR was well above what he expected for the pace, cause unclear — the shin problems in the last few weeks before the race, or the indoor track itself.** So the gap is not a clean surface offset; some unknown share of it was a bad day on compromised preparation. The honest statement for the race plan is: the gap is somewhere between the track-to-indoor-loop surface cost (unknown, probably 10–20 s/km) and the full 35–40 s/km seen last year, and a clean build this year should land nearer the low end. The ≤5:25 gate was written as a road 30 km number; it should be restated in track terms once Oct 23 and Oct 30 are in, and the sub-9 decision should treat last year's race pace as a floor set on a bad day, not as the conversion factor.
