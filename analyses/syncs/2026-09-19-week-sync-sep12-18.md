## The inversion

The Sep 10 file's load-bearing planning rule was: *"when a week has to shrink, the hard session shrinks first and the easy volume is the last thing cut."* It was written about a planning bias. This week reproduced the same error from the execution side.

What went missing: **Sun Sep 13 (12–14 km easy) and Wed Sep 16 (10–12 km easy)** — ~24 km of purely aerobic work, the exact kilometres the volume rebuild existed to deliver.

What appeared instead: **two unprescribed hard sessions**, in a week whose budget was one controlled intensity touch. Of the 33 km run Sep 14–18, roughly half was at threshold or above.

This is not a compliance complaint. It is the same structural failure the Sep 10 file diagnosed, and it matters because the stated limiter was never intensity — it was four weeks of lost aerobic volume, and easy kilometres are the only thing that treats it.

### Tue Sep 15 — prescribed "no VO2", ran VO2

4 × 4:00 with ~90 s jog recovery, deliberate and progressive:

| rep | distance | avg HR | max HR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 934 m | 153 | 164 |
| 2 | 1033 m | 164 | 175 |
| 3 | 991 m | 165 | 176 |
| 4 | 1046 m | **173** | **182** |

Reps at ~4:00–4:17/km. **10:53 above the 165 cap** (29.5% of the session) in three sustained blocks, not incidental spikes — reps 2, 3 and 4 were essentially entirely over it. 8:31 above 170, 1:32 above 180 (99% of max HR 184).

Total load looks harmless — TRIMP 77.4, suffer 47, the smallest of any real run in the window — **because the session was only 33 minutes**. That is precisely why it passed unnoticed in the weekly totals. Intensity density, not total load, is the honest readout: 25% of the session above 170.

### Thu Sep 17 — named "Tröskel", was a race

Continuous, not reps: only 3.0 min below HR 150 across 50 minutes, no recovery valleys. HR stepped up through km 2–5 and then **plateaued at 172–176 for the final 6 km**, topping out at 184 — the configured max — in the last kilometre.

- **≥160 bpm: 42.5 min = 84% of the session**
- **≥170 bpm: 30.0 min = 59%**

At **3.15 TRIMP/min this is the highest intensity density of any session in the window** (next highest: 2.59, the Sep 4 run). It packed 1.43× the load of the Sep 10 gate run into the same 50 minutes. Paces (4:28–5:37/km) look modest only because of 188 m of trail vert — read the HR.

Coming 2 days after a VO2 session and 1 day before the long run, Thu–Fri is a substantial back-to-back quality block that no version of the plan asked for.

### Fri Sep 18 — the rehearsal, read carefully

| segment | pace | GAP | avg HR | max HR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| km 0–10 | 5:23 | 5:20 | 146.4 | 162 |
| **km 10–15 (surge)** | **4:39** | **4:40** | **165.5** | **172** |
| km 15–16.7 | 5:25 | 5:17 | 163.4 | 171 |

Surge km-by-km HR: **160 → 164 → 167 → 167 → 170**.

**4:40 GAP is exactly sub-2:20 pace for 30 km.** Against the 2025 reference (4:44/km at HR 168 for 2:24:07) that is marginally faster at marginally lower HR. But:

- held for **5 km / 23 min**, not the prescribed 6–8 km;
- on a net-downhill block (41 m up / 52 m down);
- with HR **rising monotonically the whole way** and 16% of the block already ≥170 — the trace had not stabilised when the block ended;
- off legs that were one day removed from a max-HR session.

Read as a rehearsal it did its job. **Read as evidence for sub-2:20 it is not sufficient** — the block ended before the question it was asked to answer ("does this HR settle?") had an answer. The Sep 10 file's position stands unchanged: no number is set in advance, and nothing here sets one.

Load TRIMP 197.1 — the lightest of the four long/medium efforts in this window (Sep 11: 316.7; Aug 28: 274.0; Sep 4: 259.7). Eight days out that is taper-adjacent and fine.

## The race finding: hills cost pace when easy, and HR when hard

Patrik's note: *"pulsen går upp direkt när det börjar luta det minsta uppåt."* The data says this is **false at aerobic effort and true at race effort** — and the split is the useful part.

Within the easy km 0–10, detrended for time drift:

| grade | HR | speed |
|---|---|---|
| ~0% | 146.7 | 3.18 m/s |
| +2% | 146.9 | 3.03 m/s |
| +4% | 147.5 | 2.88 m/s |
| +6% | 149.4 | 2.70 m/s |

A +6% grade costs **+2.7 bpm** while pace falls 15%. That is a well-regulated uphill response — the grade is paid in pace, and the subjective impression is not supported.

Near threshold it inverts. Km 11–12 held the one real climb inside the surge (+25 m): pace fell to 5:06 while **GAP went to 4:31 — he pushed** — and HR jumped from 160 to **171**, the session's max region. At race intensity there is almost no HR headroom on a climb, so the hill gets paid **twice**, in pace and in heart rate.

**Concrete implication for Lidingöloppet.** The course carries far more vert than this run's 161 m / 16.7 km. The 4:40 GAP demonstrated on rolling road will not survive Lidingö's climbs at HR ≤ 168 if the pace is defended uphill. Conceding pace on the climbs deliberately is the only way the HR cap and the finishing time coexist — which is the same instruction the plan has always carried ("out at HR 165–168, honest through the hills, race the last 10 km"), now with a measured reason behind it.
