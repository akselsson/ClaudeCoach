# Illness week — Aug 29 → Sep 6

The Lidingöloppet block prescribed Aug 29–Sep 6 as "absorb + rebuild": easy running, a controlled-threshold Kundo on Tue Sep 1, and a 90-minute Z2 long run on Fri Sep 4 that was supposed to double as the recovery-gate check. What happened instead: almost nothing, then one hard day taken while incubating a fever.

Symptoms: fever, plus **very light coughing and no breathing heaviness**. That's essentially an above-the-neck illness with a minimal chest component — but it ran a real fever, which is the part that governs the return.

## The Sep 4 run

Patrik's own note: *"Fräscha ben men lite hög puls i början… fick ändå feeling och körde på vid segmenten men drog hem när jag började få mer och mer huvudvärk och halsont efter halva banan. Man är aldrig i bättre form än dagen före en förkylning."*

Characterized `vo2_or_race` / `race_or_hard_long` — **not** a curtailed easy run. 297 m gain over 17.2 km on technical trail, 5:49/km average, and:

- **Zones:** 38% sub-threshold (150–160), 35% threshold (160–170), 11% VO2 (>170). **84% of the run above 150 bpm.**
- **Load:** TRIMP 260 / Edwards 345 / suffer 221 — i.e. **95% of the TRIMP of the full Aug 28 shape test**, and ~3.5× the Aug 27 threshold session. The half he "only" ran cost as much as the whole hard day a week earlier.
- **Max HR 184** — equal to the configured max, hit on the km-7 climb at *6:03/km*, with a ~40 s plateau at 183–184. The Aug 28 all-out progression only reached 179.

### Why this reads as illness, not terrain

The discriminating finding is the very first minutes, before any hill:

| Time to reach | Sep 4 | Aug 28 |
|---|---|---|
| HR 140 | 0:51 | 3:26 |
| **HR 150** | **1:21** | **22:00** |
| HR 155 | 5:32 | 32:05 |
| HR 160 | 11:41 | 32:37 |

At matched grade-adjusted pace (~5:05–5:12/km) he ran **136–144 bpm on Aug 28 and 151–158 bpm on Sep 4** — a 12–18 bpm offset seven days apart. Km 1–2 were flat-ish road-equivalent (+5 m, +12 m) at 5:04 and 5:12, already at 140/151 vs 130/136 for the same work the week before. Terrain cannot explain that.

Decoupling backs it: grade-adjusted speed per bpm fell 24.6 → 22.0 → 17–19 (**−21%**) at low output, against −9% on Aug 28 *while accelerating to 4:30/km*.

What is **not** illness: the positive split itself (5:18 → 5:55/km halves) is largely 297 m of technical Ursvik trail plus deliberate segment pushes, and GAP under-models technical footing. And HR was never "still climbing at the end" — it hit its ceiling at 35 min and he backed off. That's a sensible abort, not a blow-up.

## Interpretation

1. **The max-HR 184 is a fever artefact, not a recalibration signal.** Febrile and pre-febrile states drive HR up at any workload; a "new max" reached while jogging uphill at 6:03/km is evidence of illness, not of a higher ceiling. `config/training.json` stays at 184 — do not read this session as license to raise it.
2. **The Aug 28 read still stands.** The +15 bpm easy-pace drift on the shape test was attributed to incomplete autonomic recovery from the 100-miler. Relative to Sep 4 that session now looks *healthy* — 136–144 bpm at 5:05–5:12/km. Patrik dates the "något på gång" feeling to the week of Aug 31–Sep 4, so the two are separate events. (Small caveat: very early incubation on Aug 28 can't be fully excluded, which would make that day's drift number slightly pessimistic as a fitness reading. It doesn't change the conclusion either way.)
3. **The cost is real and it is mostly the calendar, not the body.** Aug 29 – Sep 6 delivered one strength session and one hard trail day out of a prescribed easy-rebuild week. The block has lost ~10 days of aerobic rebuilding and, more importantly, the recovery gate never got its clean check — Fri Sep 4 was supposed to be that check and instead measured a fever.
4. **The engine evidence is unchanged.** Nothing here contradicts Aug 27 (threshold on-kms, no fade at D+12) or Aug 28 (near-parity with the 2025 shape test). Ten days of illness costs sharpness and freshness; it does not undo a 100-mile build. What it does cost is the *runway* to convert that engine into 30 km race pace.
