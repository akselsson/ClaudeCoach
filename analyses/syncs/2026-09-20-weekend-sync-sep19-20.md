## Saturday: read the context, not the efficiency number

The raw comparison looks like a fatigue signal and isn't one. Against a grade-adjusted pace-vs-HR regression over 17 easy/steady runs Jul 1 – Sep 18, Saturday came in **13 s/km slower than predicted** for HR 129.7 — and worse if the strides are stripped out of the average.

**That reading is confounded and should not be carried forward.** The first 7.5 km were run with the family, so the pace was set socially, not physiologically. Running 15 s/km slow at someone else's pace is not the same measurement as running 15 s/km slow at your own, and only the latter would say anything about fitness. The regression assumes self-paced running; this run wasn't.

What the run does support:

- **HR was genuinely low and well controlled** — 90.5% of the run below 140, 9.5% in 140–150, **zero time at or above 150**. The <150 ceiling was honoured with room to spare.
- **The strides were banked.** Five discrete 15–25 s bursts in the last 15 minutes, averaging 4:42–4:57/km, HR peaking 135–142. That satisfies the 4–6 strides. They're soft against a 3:58/km 5 k anchor — jog-strides rather than turnover — but at six days out, soft is the right error.
- **The discomfort was clunky, heavy legs, not a niggle.** No localised complaint. The **right hip flexor stayed silent**, consistent with every day since Sep 12.

So the honest verdict on Saturday is: **the session was cut, and cutting it cost ~6–7 km of the week's aerobic volume — but nothing about how it felt is a fitness verdict.** Heavy legs six days after a back-to-back of Thu Sep 17 (TRIMP 158, suffer 162) and Fri Sep 18 (TRIMP 197, suffer 160) is the expected reading, and the user's own note — still "some muscle soreness from thursday/friday quality" on Sunday — dates it correctly.

## Sunday: the day the numbers actually speak

Sunday is the cleaner data point of the two, because it was self-paced.

- **GAP 5:08/km at HR 142.4 — 5 s/km *faster* than the Jul–Sep line predicts.** Nearest direct match is Aug 6 (5:10/km at HR 141.7 on comparable terrain): functionally identical. Sunday to Saturday is an ~18 s/km swing in apparent efficiency in 24 hours, which is itself the clearest evidence that Saturday's number was about pacing company, not legs.
- **Pace held.** HR drift +0.76 bpm/km (negligible), first half 140.5 → second half 143.9, pace 5:12 → 5:18. No fade over 53 minutes.
- **No hangover penalty.** `2026-09-19-race-week.md` explicitly predicted HR would read "5–10 bpm high at any pace" after the party and told him to ignore it. **It didn't.** The prediction was wrong in the athlete's favour, and this is worth remembering when the same warning is applied to race day: the file also forecasts an elevated-HR race off four compromised nights. One clean data point doesn't overturn that — Mon–Wed are the heavy nights and haven't happened yet — but it does mean the alcohol penalty is a hypothesis, not an established fact, and race-day HR should be read on the day rather than pre-discounted.

**One drift to note:** the day was prescribed "easy by feel" and landed **74.9% in the 140–150 steady band**, average above the 140 easy ceiling. At 53 minutes the cost is trivial. But it is the third instance this month of the same documented pattern — running above prescription — and it is the pattern the race-week file names as the single biggest risk to Saturday. Low stakes here; not low stakes on Thursday.
